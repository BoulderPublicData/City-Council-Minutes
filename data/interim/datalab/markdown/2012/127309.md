### **B. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION PROVIDING DIRECTION ON OPTIONS FOR REDUCING DISPOSABLE CHECKOUT BAG USE IN BOULDER.**

Jamie Harkens of the Local Environmental Action Division, presented information regarding proposed plastic and paper bag use in Boulder. This program was born from public participation committees. In 2011, 33 million plastic bags were used in the City of Boulder. Removing plastic bags from residential recycle bins is costly.

Process of research included; Public outreach, researching other communities, large bag distributors, and Boulder Chamber team. Retail businesses, all food/grocery stores and convenient stores distribute bags. Staff recommended placing a fee on both plastic and paper bags and applying the ordinance to all food stores. Funds from fees could be used for bag giveaways. Upon approval of Council - next steps would be to refine analysis, conduct additional public outreach, draft an ordinance, get EAB recommendation on ordinance, and Council consideration of ordinance.

Council Member Becker asked about the legal rationale for fees.

City Attorney Carr responded that a fee can be imposed that supports a regulatory program. A tax would have to be approved by voters. A Nexus Study could save litigation in the future.

{7}------------------------------------------------

Education could also be funded with the fee.

Council Member Jones asked what other cities use the same fees for. Fee/fee vs. Ban/Fee. Has a city transitioned from fee/fee to ban/ban? Are FSC certified paper bags an option?

Ms. Harkens responded with administrative costs, bag give-aways and supporting recycling programs. Some cities did not do a Nexus Study. Ban/bans have not yet been implemented. It would be possible to have an ordinance that required FSC certified bags.

Council Member Cowles asked about including restaurants in the program.

Council Member Morzel indicated that the goal was to get people to re-use things. Regarding Nexus Study, she asked who gets hired to conduct a Nexus Study.

Council Member Wilson asked if staff had thought about an exemption for bio-degradable and compostable bags. Jamie Harkens stated there was a concern that most compostable bags would have to be disposed of in composting containers which aren't always available - this is a missing link. A fee would remind people to use cloth bags.

Council Member Karakehian asked if there had been studies on whether there was a decrease in business in the towns where the ordinances are in place. Jamie Harkens clarified that staff had no data on that.

Mayor Appelbaum would approve a ban on plastic but nothing on paper. Collecting money creates a disincentive. He felt there were other mechanisms for raising money. Collecting money requires managing it.

Council Member Morzel preferred to do a total ban as soon as possible, but acknowledged that this was not realistic. Ban/Fee for now. Not yet ready to include restaurants. She felt a set timeline to move from being disposable was urgent.

Council Member Karakehian expressed concern that the direction this was going was confusing. However, the grocery stores needed to come up with a reason for people to shop there. Ban/Fee seemed to get to the root of problem.

Ms. Harkins of Local Environmental Action Division indicated that a Nexus Study would take from 6-10 weeks.

Council Member Cowles moved, seconded by Council Member Becker to provide direction to develop an ordinance to reduce disposable check-out bag use that 1. places a fee on disposable plastic and paper check-out bags distributed through city of Boulder and 2. that it applies to all food stores in the city of Boulder. The motion carried, 7:1, with Council Member Karakehian opposed and Council Member Ageton absent. Vote taken at 9:40 PM.
