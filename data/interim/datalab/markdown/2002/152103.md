# **CITY OF BOULDER** **CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS** **July 23, 2002**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Will Toor called the regular July 23, 2002 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Toor and Councilmembers Corson, Eldridge, Havlick, Poinsatte, Riggle and Ruzzin. Councilmembers Mock and Morzel were absent.

## **2. CITIZEN PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE**

1. Randall Dubis, 1321 College Ave, 80302; Re: Macerich - encouraged the City to play hardball with Macerich. As a business owner, faithfully pays his taxes, and focuses on Customer Services. The big corporations have taken that element away.
2. Fred Smith, 505 S. Boulder Circle, #5035, 80303; re: Living Wage - in Boulder County it takes over \$1500 per month just to survive. Encouraged the City to support a living wage in Boulder.
3. Isidore Hauser, 4500 19<sup>th</sup> St., #342, 80304; re: The Living Wage Ordinance campaign. Asked Council to support this ordinance.
4. Jeff Ballagh, 1067 Raymer Lane, Louisville, 80027; Economic Student from DU. Speaking in favor of the Living Wage Ordinance. Offered to provide cost impact analysis.
5. Tracy Mott, 1395 Kalmia Ave., 80304; Member of Living Wage Coalition - Thesis Director for Jeff - supported allowing him to do his Masters thesis on this topic and asked if additional information/data could be made available to him.
6. Rob Smoke, 2445 19<sup>th</sup> St., #B, 80304; re: Crossroads - encouraged the City to look for other alternatives to a shopping mall at the Crossroads site.

### **STAFF and COUNCIL RESPONSE:** 7:05 pm

At the request of Council, City Manager Secrist acknowledged that he is the person responsible for the decisions made related to water restrictions throughout the City. Those decisions were made after receiving input from the Council and advice from City staff who are experts in water resource issues. It was also acknowledged by both Council and the Manager that the current drought is extremely severe and that the cost to the community, while necessary, is high.

- 10. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE No. 7216 SUBMITTING TO THE QUALIFIED ELECTORS OF THE CITY OF BOULDER AT A SPECIAL MUNICIPAL ELECTION TO BE HELD ON TUESDAY, THE 5<sup>TH</sup> DAY OF NOVEMBER, 2002, THE QUESTION OF AUTHORIZING THE CITY COUNCIL TO CONTINUE A 0.15 CENTS ON EACH DOLLAR SALES AND USE TAX APPROVED BY THE VOTERS IN 1997 THROUGH DECEMBER 31, 2014, FOR PUBLIC SAFETY PURPOSES, CONCERNING FIRE, POLICE, COURT AND PROSECUTION SERVICES; GIVING APPROVAL FOR THE COLLECTION, RETENTION, AND EXPENDITURE OF THE FULL TAX PROCEEDS AND ANY EARNINGS RELATING THERETO NOTWITHSTANDING ANY STATE REVENUE OR EXPENDITURE LIMITATION; SETTING FORTH AN EFFECTIVE DATE; SETTING FORTH THE BALLOT TITLE; AND PROVIDING FURTHER DETAILS IN RELATION TO THE FOREGOING. - 9:26 P.M.**

The public hearing was opened:

1. Jim Crain, 1027 Pineknoll Dr., 80517; Spoke in support of the extension of the public safety tax.
2. Jim Hill, 450 Oakwood Place, 80304; Spoke in support of maintaining the quality of public safety both through Police and Fire. Supported extension of the Public Safety Tax.
3. Karen Graffenberger, 2035 Norwood Avenue, 80304; Spoke of the benefits to the community from the existing public safety tax - encouraged placement on the November Ballot.
4. Barb Odbert, 5360 Cypress Drive, 80303; Nothing has changed since the tax was approved the first time and urged that Council support this ordinance.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

{5}------------------------------------------------

Councilmember Ruzzin moved, seconded by Poinsatte to adopt Ordinance No. 7216 submitting to the qualified electors of the City of Boulder at a special municipal election to be held on Tuesday, the 5<sup>th</sup> day of November, 2002, the question of authorizing the City Council to continue a 0.15 cents on each dollar sales and use tax approved by the voters in 1997 through December 31, 2014, for public safety purposes, concerning fire, police, court and prosecution services; giving approval for the collection, retention, and expenditure of the full tax proceeds and any earnings relating thereto notwithstanding any state revenue or expenditure limitation; setting forth an effective date; setting forth the ballot title; and providing further details in relation to the foregoing. The motion carried 5-1, Riggle opposed. Havlick, Mock and Morzel absent.

- 11. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE No. 7217 SUBMITTING TO THE QUALIFIED ELECTORS OF THE CITY OF BOULDER (THE “CITY”) AT A SPECIAL COORDINATED MUNICIPAL ELECTION TO BE HELD ON NOVEMBER 5, 2002, TO INCREASE THE CITY’S OPEN SPACE SALES AND USE TAX BY 0.15 CENTS PER DOLLAR STARTING JANUARY 1, 2003 THROUGH DECEMBER 31, 2018, AND TO PLEDGE THE REVENUES DERIVED FROM SUCH TAX FOR OPEN SPACE PURPOSES; GIVING APPROVAL FOR THE COLLECTION, RETENTION, AND EXPENDITURE OF THE FULL TAX PROCEEDS AND ANY EARNINGS RELATING THERETO NOTWITHSTANDING ANY STATE REVENUE OR EXPENDITURE LIMITATION; SETTING FORTH AN EFFECTIVE DATE; SETTING FORTH THE BALLOT TITLE; AND PROVIDING FURTHER DETAILS IN RELATION TO THE FOREGOING. - 9:40 P.M.**

The public hearing was opened:

1. David Paddock, 3665, 61<sup>st</sup>, 80301; Spoke in favor of the Open Space Tax.
2. Jim Crain, 1027 Pineknoll Drive, 80517; Spoke in support of this ordinance noting the importance of continued preservation of our natural resources.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Ruzzin moved, seconded by Havlick to adopt Ordinance No. 7217 submitting to the qualified electors of the City of Boulder (the “City”) at a special coordinated municipal election to be held on November 5, 2002, to increase the City’s Open Space sales and use tax by 0.15 cents per dollar starting January 1, 2003 through December 31, 2018, and to pledge the revenues derived from such tax for open space purposes; giving approval for the collection, retention, and expenditure of the full tax proceeds and any earnings relating thereto notwithstanding any state revenue or expenditure limitation; setting forth an effective date; setting forth the ballot title; and providing further details in relation to the foregoing. The motion carried 7-0, Mock and Morzel absent.

- 12. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE No. 7220 DESIGNATING THE STRUCTURE AND A PORTION OF ITS SITE LOCATED AT 845 WALNUT, ALSO KNOWN AS THE ANDERSON-MARTIN HOUSE, AS AN INDIVIDUAL LANDMARK UNDER THE CITY OF BOULDER HISTORIC PRESERVATION CODE. THE HEARING ON THIS ITEM WILL BE HELD UNDER THE QUASI-JUDICIAL HEARING PROCEDURES OF CHAPTER 1-3 B.R.C., 1981. APPLICANT: MARY LEE AND CHARLES E. WITHERS.**

{6}------------------------------------------------

The City Clerk swore in all parties on this issue.

Deon Wolfenbarger - long range Planner for the City of Boulder reviewed the background and actions proposed for this issue.

Council was asked to disclose any *ex-parte* communications regarding this item.

Councilmember Corson disclosed that sometime in the 1990's he received a call from the owner's attorney asking about the historical significance of this site.

There being no speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Eldridge moved, seconded by Corson to adopt Ordinance No. 7220 designating the structure and a portion of its site located at 845 Walnut, also known as the Anderson-Martin House, as an individual landmark under the City of Boulder Historic Preservation Code. The hearing on this item will be held under the quasi-judicial hearing procedures of Chapter 1-3 B.R.C., 1981. Applicant: Mary Lee and Charles E. Withers. The motion carried 7-0, Mock and Morzel absent.
