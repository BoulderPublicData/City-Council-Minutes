# **CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS**

**Tuesday, May 5, 2009**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Appelbaum called the regular May 5, 2009 council meeting to order at 6:05 p.m. in the council chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Appelbaum, Deputy Mayor Gray, and Council Members Ageton, Cowles, Espinoza, Morzel, Osborne and Wilson.

### **A. Presentation of *Sister City Volunteer of the Year Award to Harumi Kato.***

Mayor Appelbaum presented Harumi Kato with the Sister City Volunteer of the Year Award for her efforts coordinating local volunteers associated with the Sakura Project and the Yamagata Sister City committee to plant cherry trees in Boulder.

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE - 6:10 p.m.**

1. Lenna Kottke, Executive Director of Special Transit, addressed the opportunity to provide additional services with grant funds and urged adoption of item 3E.
2. Sam Alschuler spoke regarding Orchard Grove and the angst that exists in the Park given the uncertainty of its future with the continued rent increases.
3. Seth Brigham spoke to the suggestion of creating a living wage for council but why not a living wage for the community?
4. James McClements spoke of the proposed collaboration with CU and BVSD. He suggested a way to bring together BVSD, the City and residents to address traffic problems related to Foothills Elementary School.
5. Robert Webb, neighbor of Foothills Elementary School, spoke in support of developing a strategic and inclusive process for a Foothills Elementary safety plan.
6. Stephen Keenan voiced support for concerns expressed by the Foothills Elementary parents and neighbors. He suggested forming a separate school district for the city.
7. Rob Smoke commended staff for the good job it does (noted 28<sup>th</sup> and Pearl as an example). He urged finding a way to save libraries; possibly cutting salaries is a way to keep the community whole.
8. Robert Sharpe spoke of the challenges facing Boulder due to the changing values of the city's elected officials and corrupt city staff.
9. Sarah Schulte a Foothills Elementary parent indicated how grateful they are to have such a wonderful school. The frustration comes from a process that was no process. There was no community input allowed in making a safety decision which will result in the removal of over ½ of the green area without considering other options.
10. Callie Weiant spoke regarding a work plan for the revitalization of University Hill. She voiced her support for the initiative as there is tremendous potential for improvements.
11. Morgan Rogers representing PLAN Boulder County spoke in support of the direction of the Climate Action Plan but would like to hear how the plan will address the goals before supporting a tax increase.

{1}------------------------------------------------

12. Michael Rowe spoke to restoring the Pioneer Train. He asked council to take action to support this effort.
13. Lynn Segal spoke to the need to delay the opening of the Comanche III Coal Plant.

### **Staff Response: - 6:55 p.m.**

City Manager Brautigam responded as follows:

- 1) A Pioneer Passenger Rail Resolution will be coming for council consideration on May 19.
- 2) Regarding Foothills Elementary, Ms Brautigam noted that staff has met with representatives from the School District who indicated that they would be meeting with neighboring residents.

### **City Council Response:**

Council Member Ageton addressed Mr. Sharpe asking that he not call staff corrupt unless there is evidence of that, in which case it should be brought to the attention of the City Manager.

Council Member Osborne spoke of non-profit work going forward to try and provide emergency assistance to Orchard Grove residents.

Council Member Cowles spoke regarding Foothills Elementary and clarified that the School District is not required to follow the city's planning regulations. He was pleased the BVSD was going to meet with residents. He also expressed his disappointment in the weekly self righteous speeches from Mr. Sharpe which then turns into false allegations and out right lies. He expressed that this having this occur week after week, was not appropriate.

## **5. PUBLIC HEARINGS:**

- A. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ADOPT ORDINANCE No. 7658 AMENDING THE REQUIREMENTS RELATED TO THE PROTECTION OF WETLANDS, INCLUDING SECTIONS 9-3-9, “WETLANDS PROTECTION,” 9-16-1, “GENERAL DEFINITIONS,” AND 4-20-53, “WETLAND PERMIT AND MAP REVISION FEES,” B.R.C. 1981, BY REPEALING, REENACTING AND ADOPTING BY REFERENCE THE WETLANDS MAPS OF THE CITY OF BOULDER AND THE WETLANDS FUNCTIONAL EVALUATIONS AND SETTING FORTH RELATED DETAILS. – 7:12 P.M.**

Presentation by Beverly Johnson, Environmental Planner with a recommendation of adoption of proposed changes to:

- 1. Overlay district regulations
- 2. Regulatory maps
- 3. Permit and boundary change fees

{4}------------------------------------------------

The public hearing was opened:

1. Sam Alschuler, Orchard Grove resident, indicated that the 5-acre parcel is not covered by the Wetlands Ordinance because the ditch was man made (note – over 100 years ago).
2. Barrett Weisz addressed the Greenbelt Meadows mosquitoes issue. He noted that the city is incredibly lucky to have an extraordinary staff that has been completely responsive in moving this forward.
3. Barb Kostanick spoke as an effected property owner urging council to adopt the ordinance. It had been a long and painful process over 4 years. This would be a huge step forward.
4. Gail Gordon expressed that she is still concerned that the “right to build or rebuild in perpetuity for current owners and future owners” is still not in the ordinance.
5. David W. Crumpacker, Shadow Creek Condominiums, requested that a permit not be required to rebuild a structure with its existing footprint.
6. Buff Elting, 25 year resident, stressed that people who live by the creek value the aesthetics of this neighborhood. He also requested the ability to rebuild.
7. Mary Nakashian, Shadow Creek Condominiums, thanked the Planning Board and Planning staff for all the hard work in drafting the proposed Ordinance. However, she agreed with previous speakers’ comments that no permit should be required and regarding the right to rebuild for current and future owners in perpetuity.
8. Phoebe Greyson also asked that clear language be added to clarify the right to rebuild in perpetuity for current and future owners.
9. Bob Rubino, Arapahoe resident, echoed concerns of other neighbors. All of the neighbors support protecting the creek but it has become impossible to do anything in their back yards. He requested council not adopt the Ordinance in its current form.

The public hearing was closed at 8:27 p.m.

Council Member Wilson moved, seconded by Osborne to adopt Ordinance No. 7658 amending the requirements related to the protection of wetlands, including Sections 9-3-9, “Wetlands Protection,” 9-16-1, “General Definitions,” and 4-20-53, “Wetland Permit and Map Revision Fees,” B.R.C. 1981, by repealing, reenacting and adopting by reference the wetlands maps of the city of Boulder and the wetlands functional evaluations and setting forth related details. The motion carried 8:0. – 8:40 p.m.

### **C. CONTINUATION OF SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ADOPT ORDINANCE No. 7552, ADOPTING AND APPROVING AN INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN BOULDER COUNTY, THE CITY AND COUNTY OF BROOMFIELD, THE CITIES OF BOULDER, LAFAYETTE, LONGMONT, AND LOUISVILLE, AND THE TOWNS OF ERIE AND SUPERIOR, RELATING TO THE MANAGEMENT OF HAZARDOUS MATERIALS. – 8:45 P.M.**

Presentation by Jonathan Koehn with additional comments from Boulder County Commissioner Will Toor.

Commissioner Toor responded to the question from Mayor Appelbaum regarding no cost to unincorporated County residents, explaining that the County deals with many services countywide (some of which are a benefit to city residents at no cost) and is faced with finding the appropriate balance between service provision and cost. He further indicated that the goal of having proper disposal of hazardous waste materials is to encourage participation.

There being no speakers the public hearing was closed.

Council Member Gray moved, seconded by Morzel to adopt substitute Ordinance No. 7552 adopting and approving an Intergovernmental Agreement between Boulder County, the City and County of Broomfield, the Cities of Boulder, Lafayette, Longmont, and Louisville, and the Towns of Erie and Superior, relating to the management of hazardous materials; and its Exhibit A found in Attachment B. The motion carried 8:0. – 9:06 p.m.
