# **CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS**

**Tuesday, October 27, 2009**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Appelbaum called the regular October 27, 2009 council meeting to order at 6:05p.m. in the council chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Appelbaum, Deputy Mayor Gray and Council Members Ageton, Cowles, Espinoza, Morzel, Osborne and Wilson.

### **A. NATIONAL WOMEN'S HALL OF FAME DECLARATION – 6:05 P.M.**

Mayor Appelbaum read a declaration honoring Susan Solomon for her recent induction into the National Women's Hall of Fame for her work on climate issues and declared October 11, 2009 as Susan Solomon Day in the City of Boulder.

### **B. JANET DRISKELL TURNER OUTSTANDING ADULT LEARNER AWARD - 6:06 P.M.**

Mayor Appelbaum and Diana Sherry honored Sito Martinez-Arias with the Janet Driskell Turner Outstanding Adult Learner Award.

Mayor Appelbaum then invited Audrey Debarros, Director of U.S. 36 Commuting Solutions, to speak. Ms. Debarros indicated the U.S. 36 environmental impact study (EIS) would conclude this year. She urged council to help support the Transportation Tiger Grant by sending letters of support to the state legislature.

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE - 6:19 p.m.**

1. Stephen Sparn, Architect and Planner for the Junior Academy, asked council to adopt the Junior Academy as approved by the Planning Board.
2. Jerry Allen spoke to the Vista Village issue and asked why there was no agreement preventing Mr. Miller from re-erecting a fence at Vista Village.
3. Seth Brigham spoke in opposition to the Junior Academy Area Plan item and urged Council Member Ageton to recuse from voting on the item.
4. Elizabeth Allen spoke to the Junior Academy Area Plan and asked council to support what the residents wanted.

7. Rick Johnson, Counsel for the owner of the Junior Academy property, spoke in opposition to the comment that the area plan represents spot zoning. He referred to an FAR comparison chart distributed to Council Members and urged Council's adoption of the area plan without

it being further politicized.

8. Leanne Eckelberg, a CU Student and head of the University COPIRG program, spoke to the issue of affordable housing and homelessness in Boulder. She indicated students are available for volunteer work and supported finding affordable solutions for homelessness and better solutions to the camping laws in Boulder.
9. Peter Richards provided a handout for City Council and spoke to the Broadway/Euclid Transportation Project. He questioned how funding was made available for such an “undefined” project. He asked for a response about transportation projects being fully vetted in the community.
10. Ron Geary spoke to what he perceived as the skewed land use process in Boulder that he felt seemed to always favor the developer. He urged Council not to pass the Junior Academy Area Plan and to refer it back to staff.

19. Doris Hass spoke in support of keeping the branch libraries open until 9 p.m.

### **City Manager Response:7:08 p.m.**

1. Ms. Brautigam thanked Leanne Eckelberg for her comments and noted that the HRC recently had a meeting to discuss homelessness in Boulder. She offered to provide Council with clear information about camping and ticketing of the homeless in Boulder.
2. Regarding Ms. Carlat’s comments on noxious air quality Ms. Brautigam indicated staff would be happy to look into this if directed to do so by council.
3. Regarding the trees at Willow Ridge, she indicated that out of the 8 trees removed, 7 of them should not have been removed. She acknowledged this revealed a flaw in the process and in the future, a written scope of work would be done to ensure City requirements are met. In addition, staff would be working with FEMA to determine the type of re-vegetation that could be incorporated.

Mayor Appelbaum commented that this was a first reading item and questions should be asked for the purpose of receiving clarification at second reading.

Karen Rahn, Housing and Human Services Director, introduced the item and explained that this was the second phase of the Inclusionary zoning project.

Michelle Allen then reviewed what the proposed Inclusionary Housing ordinance would include.

Council Member Espinoza asked about the program's impact on the middle income market.

Council Member Morzel asked about whether the City could reach 100% so there was no affordability gap (i.e. could 20% of total square footage of a project be affordable instead of just 20% of all units).

Council Member Cowles clarified that no gap would be closed with respect to the smaller projects (less than 5 units). Staff noted that unit sizes would be discussed during the 1<sup>st</sup> Quarter of 2010 as part of phase III. Structural changes to land use regulations (density) would begin with the Planning department in the coming year.

Council Member Ageton raised concern about different definitions of housing units in the code. She also raised concern about separating mobile home parks from affordable housing and noted that integration/synchronization could be lost. Ms. Rahn indicated that the two could still be integrated but staff wanted to move it up due to recent mobile home related issues.

Mayor Appelbaum referenced his Hotline questions on this topic and asked when administrative rules would be discussed by Council.

Council Member Osborne raised the issue of congregate care facilities.

Deputy Mayor Gray asked whether there could be an affordable housing trust via deed restriction. She then asked whether ADU's and OAU's would be subject to inclusionary housing ordinances? Staff responded that they would not.

Council Member Espinoza raised the following:

- Agenda pages 41, 46 b,1 and pg 47, 2 she requested examples of what that would look like;
- in C,3, she asked if developers would get equity for finishing the space;
- Agenda page 51, C she asked for examples of what would be eligible and how staff could ensure units get upgraded over time as there was limited equity incentive.
- Agenda page 54, b, in terms of better quality she asked how the City Manager would make that decision.
- Agenda page 55, e and 56, 9-13-10, she indicated it made no sense to build fewer large units when more units are needed.
