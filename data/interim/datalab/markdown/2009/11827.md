# **CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS**

**Tuesday, August 18, 2009**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Appelbaum called the regular August 18, 2009 council meeting to order at 6:05pm in the council chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Appelbaum, Deputy Mayor Gray and Council Members Ageton, Cowles, Espinoza, Morzel, Osborne and Wilson.

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE**

1. Mark Young pooling time with Drew Dolan and Craig Lindsley spoke to his struggle with the Planning department regarding Flatirons Theatre noting the use issue was keeping him from moving forward.
2. Seth Brigham spoke to his platform as a city council candidate and specifically addressed Planning Board decisions.
3. Rob Smoke noted that the council had just paid a visit to Boulder Reservoir.
4. Warren Hultquist representing 'Leave My Home Alone' spoke in support of further analysis of the alternative suggestions made by Mayor Appelbaum. Specifically a tool on the web site where people could determine actual impacts to their property.
5. Jerry Allen spoke regarding the Vista Village fence and questioned why no legal action had been taken against Mr. Miller after council direction in March.
6. Jessica Sandler regarding the East Boulder Community Park - expressed concern that staff used e-mail to discredit the proposal by misrepresenting it and requested an investigation and then a charette with all the stakeholders to find a solution.
7. Ne Ray Key regarding East Boulder Community Park - expressed alarm over 10,000 vehicles on 55th and whether that could be accommodated. She asked if that was what we want for Boulder Neighborhoods. Requested that council look one more time at the number of parking spaces previously approved and encourage starting with a lower number. After time additional surveys could utilized to justify any increases.
8. Tanya Petty spoke to Vista Village, asking for help in identifying who could help them pass a law that would help mobile home owners. Expressed frustration that nothing had been done regarding adverse possession or eminent domain.
9. Elizabeth Allen also spoke to Vista Village, supported her husbands comments about compatible development and the need for a more restrictive approach.
10. Steve Baust speaking to East Boulder Recreation Center – it was clear to him that there were a number of citizens that think there are good alternatives and don't think they were heard. He supported the design charette approach in resolving the issue.
11. Stephen Keenan spoke to the 30<sup>th</sup> Street project noting that Federal dollars could be used for other projects rather than destroying trees. He also spoke to new battery technology on [www.dollarcollapse.com](http://www.dollarcollapse.com) by Ceramatec and encouraged council to work with Xcel to utilize the new technology.

### **B. ITEMS RELATED TO 6400 ARAPAHOE, RECYCLE ROW, AND CHAPTER 3-10, "TRASH TAX," B.R.C. 1981: - 7:55 P.M.**

1. **CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO DIRECT THE CITY MANAGER TO PURCHASE THE PROPERTY AT 6400 ARAPAHOE AVE.**
2. **CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO AUTHORIZE THE CITY MANAGER TO APPROVE AN INTERGOVERNMENTAL AGREEMENT WITH BOULDER COUNTY TO FUND THE PURCHASE OF 6400 ARAPAHOE AVE.**

{4}------------------------------------------------

3. **SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF FOUR ORDINANCES AMENDING SECTION 3-10-2, “IMPOSITION OF OCCUPATION TAX,” B.R.C. 1981, RAISING THE RATES OF THE TRASH TAX.**
4. **CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ADOPT A RESOLUTION DECLARING THE CITY OF BOULDER OFFICIAL INTENT TO REIMBURSE BOULDER COUNTY FOR \$5,000,000 WITH THE PROCEEDS OF A FUTURE BOND ISSUE THAT WILL OCCUR IN THE FOURTH QUARTER OF 2009 OR THE FIRST QUARTER OF 2010.**

City Manager Brautigam provided a brief introduction of the item and thanked council and staff for its efforts on this issue.

Elizabeth Vasatka spoke to the infrastructure needed to meet Boulder’s zero waste goals. The proposed purchase of 6400 Arapahoe would provide two of the 5 identified facilities required to meet future needs. She reported that the environmental consultants had completed their work and there were no on-site contamination issues. Staff recommended council purchase the 6400 Arapahoe site. Further, staff recommended structuring the financing to accept an upfront loan from the County for the purchase, and allow the City Manager to negotiate the terms of the loan via an Intergovernmental Agreement. She then spoke to the staff recommendation for option 4 to increase the trash tax to the maximum rate.

Commissioner Toor spoke on behalf of the County Commissioners and confirmed recent County action to approve the Intergovernmental Agreement. The County also appropriated funds to loan the city for the purchase of the 6400 Arapahoe property.

The public hearing was opened:

1. Dr. Oakleigh Thorne, Founder of the Thorne Ecological Institute, indicated the Institute had not taken any stance on the property, which was misrepresented about a month ago. He spoke in support of the purchase.
2. Spenser Villwock, Interim Executive Director for the Center for Resource Conservation, spoke in support of the purchase of the 6400 Arapahoe site.
3. Judy Renfroe, a neighbor near the property, raised concern about deconstruction transfer and the noise associated with that process.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Council Member Morzel moved, seconded by Gray to 1) direct the City Manager to purchase the property at 6400 Arapahoe Avenue; 2) to authorize the City Manager to approve an Intergovernmental Agreement with Boulder County to fund the purchase of 6400 Arapahoe; 3) to direct the City Manager to structure the financing for the property acquisition generally consistent with Scenario B in Section XII C, which would provide sufficient cash equity to consider selling a portion of the property in the future; 4) to approve trash tax Ordinance No. 7678, increasing the rates of the trash tax to the maximum level; and 5) to adopt Resolution No. 1060 to reimburse funds to the City or the County or a combination thereof for capital expenditures with the proceeds of the city’s future tax-exempt borrowing. The motion carried unanimously, 8:0. – 8:55 p.m.

### **C. CONTINUATION OF FIRST READING AND CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ORDER PUBLISHED BY TITLE ONLY AN ORDINANCE AMENDING THE LAND USE CODE (TITLE 9, B.R.C.) REQUIREMENTS RELATED TO THE RESIDENTIAL LOW 1 (RL-1) ZONE DISTRICT AS PART OF THE COMPATIBLE DEVELOPMENT IN SINGLE-FAMILY NEIGHBORHOODS PROJECT. *THE PUBLIC HEARING ON THIS ITEM WAS HELD ON AUGUST 4, 2009.* – 8:57 P.M.**

Mayor Appelbaum indicated there was a motion on the floor from the August 4 council meeting:

Council Member Morzel moved, seconded by Gray to direct staff to return at second reading with a revised ordinance that reflects the staff recommendation in the July 9, 2009 Planning Board memo. This motion was taken off the floor.

Susan Richstone, Long Range Planning Manager indicated the Ordinance in Council's August 18 packet included all aspects of the compatible development tools to make it easier for council to remove items it did not wish to move forward.

Julie Johnston then reviewed the first reading questions and responses received noting that there were 7 items that would require additional analysis.

Council Member Osborne asked about the definition of uninhabitable space on page 53 and the reference to a parking facility located completely below grade. Staff indicated the below grade parking facility was already contained in the definition.

Council Member Wilson raised concern about 2<sup>nd</sup> floor width and asked if there was enough analysis to know about possible unintended consequence as many people could be impacted.

Council then reviewed each area of the ordinance in detail:

##### **1) Affected Properties:**

Mayor Appelbaum spoke to the RMX-1 Zone asking that additional analysis come back at second reading which could help avoid over burdening certain properties or leaving a large loophole in the RMX-1 zone. Council Member Espinoza Angelique suggested a second ordinance for the RMX-1 district to address its uniqueness.

There was general council agreement to include all single-family housing in the city.

##### **2) Bulk Plane:**

Council Member Osborne wanted to see more discussion at second reading about how second stories would work. Council Member Ageton raised concern about how the bulk plane interfaced with the solar ordinance.

There was general council agreement to include bulk planes as staff recommended.

{6}------------------------------------------------

##### **3) Side Wall Articulation:**

There was general agreement that further discussion would be needed at second reading on how to best utilize side wall articulation.

##### **4) Building Coverage: - 10:40 p.m.**

Council Member Cowles suggested under “exemptions” that the 150 sq. ft. for an additional porch not in the front yard be increased to 300 sq. ft. Council Member Wilson suggested they be as large as possible for such things as green houses. There were mixed opinions regarding the 35 vs. 30 percent coverage allowance.

Council Member Espinoza asked for a chart showing the trend line of setbacks and lots. There was concern that the graph changed when an accessory structure was added.

Council Member Cowles moved, seconded by Ageton to suspend the rules and continue the meeting at 11:02 p.m. The motion carried unanimously, 8:0.

##### **5) FAR:**

Mayor Appelbaum noted that basements and virtual floors had to be dealt with if using FARs. There was concern that people would scrape and build basements to achieve square footage. Council Members Wilson and Ageton indicated they would support exploring the Mayor’s previous hotline ideas, excluding FARs.

Council Member Osborne spoke to the community survey noting that two-thirds of the respondents agreed some sort of proportionality was needed regarding house size and lot size.

There was general council agreement to continue exploring options relating to FARs.

Abe Barge of Winter & Company indicated the majority of the communities they had worked with had ended up using FARs. The ones who did not were utilizing a more extensive design review process.

Mayor Applebaum indicated he would like some limited analysis of the 35% lot coverage with a .55 FAR and implications of doing that. He would also like to see some limited analysis of the bulk plane and rear yard setback. He also suggested utilizing a smaller envelope without an FAR.

Council Member Morzel moved, seconded by Gray, to introduce and order published by title only Ordinance No.7684 in Attachment A on page 33, incorporating the language on pages 14 and 15 of the memo regarding side yard bulk plane and side yard wall articulation. In addition, providing direction to staff to bring back language on second reading to address: eliminating FAR averaging, including virtual floors, and other additional analysis requested by council. The motion carried 5:3; Ageton, Espinoza and Wilson opposed.

{7}------------------------------------------------

Mayor Appelbaum indicated an interest in seeing a greater building coverage and greater FAR and what that would look like in terms of impacts, perhaps 35% building coverage and .5 FAR, to see what happens when those larger numbers are used. Council Member Osborne supported looking at this analysis.

Mayor Appelbaum also asked for some limited analysis of tightening the bulk planes, rear yard limitations as a trade off for some of the other tools (side yard articulation and FARs) as a quick look at this could help determine impacts.

Deputy Mayor Gray indicated that she would like to see exemptions included for accessibility purposes (i.e. elevators) and the same for a covered accessory structure.

Regarding variances, Council Member Ageton raised concern that these may be difficult to address at the current time with so many other items on the table.

Council Member Osborne raised concern about the 60-day implementation period and the number of variances that may be requested. Mayor Appelbaum agreed with Council Member Osborne's comments.
