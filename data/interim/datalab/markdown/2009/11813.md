# **BOULDER CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS** **February 3, 2009** **6:00 p.m.**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor McGrath called the regular February 3, 2009 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:05 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Present were: Mayor McGrath, Deputy Mayor Gray, and Council Members, Ageton, Appelbaum, Cowles, Espinoza, Morzel, Osborne and Wilson.

### **A. Council discussion regarding revised agenda.**

Council Member Wilson moved, seconded by Ageton, to remove item 11B from the agenda and bring it back on February 17. The motion failed 3:6, Ageton, Espinoza and Wilson in support.

Council Member Wilson made a motion to pull item 11B, the appointment of the Mayor, given the pending resignation of Mayor McGrath. Both Council Members Wilson and Ageton spoke to the need for time to process what is an important decision that should allow enough time for community input. Council Member Ageton suggested that the Charter Committee consider an amendment to the process of selecting a Mayor. Mayor McGrath then addressed why he supported moving forward with an appointment that evening to maintain continuity in a number of areas.

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE -- 6:20 p.m.**

Given that more than 40 speakers were signed up for this portion of the meeting the Mayor announced that speakers would be limited to 2 mins. per person.

### **Staff Response:**

1. Regarding Vista Village, City Manager Brautigam clarified that there had been a presentation at the last council meeting and that staff continued to support its recommendation of the alternative path.
2. Regarding the Martin Acres street painting project, City Manager Brautigam indicated it was a very compelling project and that the city was looking to support the project moving forward in June as a pilot with the hope of assisting the neighborhood in addressing city regulations as well.

### **Council Response:**

1. Council Member Morzel spoke to the Vista Village issue, expressing concern that the city is setting a precedent of not fighting to re-open an already established path of over thirty years.

City Manager Brautigam clarified that the fiscal impacts previously estimated did not include costs beyond that of litigation such as the actual cost of acquiring the property and that it would likely be as much if not more to proceed with litigation. She indicated that since there was a viable and safe route available it was not necessary to litigate the fence closure. However, if council wished to take a different direction, that needed to be communicated to staff.

### **B. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO PROVIDE DIRECTION TO STAFF ON NEXT STEPS IN PLANNING FOR THE POTENTIAL REVITALIZATION OF THE UNIVERSITY HILL BUSINESS DISTRICT. - 7:30 P.M.**

Presentation by Ruth McHeyser and Charles Zucker of the Planning Department.

New Hill Company presentation:

Steven Walsh and Gordon Riggle presented information on the Hill Commercial Context Study (HCCS) and made the following points:

- Make University Hill a priority goal
- Use the HCCS study as a starting point
- Ask Planning staff to begin zoning analysis to promote business incentives.

{4}------------------------------------------------

The public hearing was opened:

1. Nicholas Nagle felt the cart was being placed before the horse. It was not yet clear what the community wanted for this area. He indicated that there needed to be more community discussion.
2. Nick Johansson, member of the City Manager's Advisory Committee of Students (CMACS) encouraged a process which included an open dialogue with the entire community.
3. Eva Hueber, coordinator for Student Neighborhood Relations for the University, spoke in support of the proposal.
4. Jane Stoyva, coordinator of University Hill Neighborhood Association spoke in support of the New Hill Company and its proposal.
5. Tracey Jennings, CU faculty member and Hill resident spoke in support of the HCCS study and encouraged moving forward with the study as a starting place. Students describe the Hill as the Dump; party central for 18 to 20 year olds.
6. Mary Street, 10 year Hill resident and former University Hill General Improvement District (UHGID) board member spoke to the conditions as not dirty but run down.
7. Natalie Smithson, Greek community public relations spokesperson, indicated they are concerned that the Hill is currently "sketch central and very, very shady" which means it is not safe, dirty. They are also concerned that a redevelopment needs to be a collaboration that will welcome students, faculty and residents.
8. Bill Shrum, Ship City on the Hill owner, looking for ways to express the urgency of the need to redevelop this area. Not the social impact of the HCCS study, even beyond.
9. Elizabeth Allen expressed that University Hill is just fine. She does not want to see four story buildings. Leave it with a little renovation.
10. David Miller, Chair of UHCD, informed council of its unanimous support for change request incentives for redevelopment and specific focus on addressing parking and alcohol issues.
11. Pastor Ron Roschke from University Lutheran Church expressed concern regarding increasing trends towards violence and assaults in this area. University Hill redevelopment must be a priority to improve the city's soul on the Hill.
12. Tim Plass, speaking as an individual, agreed that there are issues such as alcohol that need to be addressed. He raised concern that the context study might threaten the ability to landmark the Hill District both locally and nationally.
13. Wesley Robison provided a student view of history on the Hill. During his time as a CU student there was consensus that redevelopment needed to be addressed but where is additional parking the right answer. Keep moving forward.
14. Lynn Segal spoke to the need for green businesses on the Hill, a little grocery store and a sock store. She suggested an area plan with parking and a shuttle service.
15. Zane Blackmer owns a building on Broadway on the Hill but overall felt the comprehensive study done by the New Hill Company was extensive and should be considered carefully. He raised concern about Historic District designation.
16. Evan Ravitz addressed council regarding history of another time when the city underwent a Hill Renovation about ten years prior, he then approached the council on the dais, was requested to leave and was then escorted out by a police officer.

Mayor McGrath apologized for the disruption and explained why the council did not allow the general public to approach the dais.

{5}------------------------------------------------

Council Member Morzel moved, seconded by Appelbaum to approve the staff recommended motion on page 2 of the agenda memo as follows:

Motion directing staff to move forward on planning for the revitalization of the Uni-Hill Business District as follows:

#### A. Initiate a short-term effort to be completed by June 2009:

1. Refine the draft problem statement, goals and objectives (see page 15 for initial draft) based on Planning Board's December 18, 2008 feedback and City Council's feedback February 3, 2009 to provide direction for the work plan in item 4 below.
2. Continue with the work underway to address use/alcohol issues (scheduled for an April 2009 City Council study session).
3. Make minor changes to the Land Use Code for presentation to Planning Board for recommendation and City Council for action including:
  - Exempt all below grade space from the Floor Area Ratio (FAR) calculations (currently 50 % of habitable below grade space is calculated in the allowable FAR).
  - Consider minor changes to the Business Main Street (BMS) zone and the University Hill General Improvement District (UHGID) boundary
4. Prepare a work plan and schedule for a comprehensive revitalization strategy for direction from Planning Board and City Council, that includes those key issues identified by Planning Board, listed on page 3 of this memo. This would require consulting with the New Hill Company and other stakeholders.
- B. Initiate development of a comprehensive revitalization strategy, process and schedule, as developed in item d-4 above (to be initiated in 2010).

Council Member Ageton offered a friendly amendment to amend the recommendation to state no later than 2010. The friendly amendment was accepted.

Vote was taken on the motion as amended. The motion carried 9:0. – 9:44 p.m.

Washington Village II Site Review #LUR2008-00083: Redevelopment of the existing Washington Elementary School Site as a mixed-use co-housing community consisting of 33 dwelling units, approximately 2,950 square feet of commercial/office space, and accessory co-housing facilities on the three-acre site zoned both Residential High Density – 2 (RH-2) and Residential Low Density – 1 (RL -1).
