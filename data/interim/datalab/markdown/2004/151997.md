# **CITY OF BOULDER COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS**

**November 30, 2004**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Ruzzin called the special November 30, 2004 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Councilmembers, Bohannan, Eldridge, Gray, McGrath, Riggle, Ruzzin, Stoakes and Toor. Councilmember Schultheiss was absent.

## **ORDER OF BUSINESS**

## **2. PUBLIC HEARINGS: - 6:00p.m.**

### **A. CONSIDERATION OF THE FOLLOWING ITEMS RELATING TO THE 2005 BUDGET:**

- 1. PUBLIC HEARING ON THE PROPOSED 2005 CITY OF BOULDER BUDGET; AND**
- 2. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 7403 WHICH ADOPTS A BUDGET FOR THE CITY OF BOULDER, COLORADO, FOR THE FISCAL YEAR COMMENCING ON THE FIRST DAY OF JANUARY, 2005 AND ENDING ON THE LAST DAY OF DECEMBER, 2005, AND SETTING FORTH DETAILS IN RELATION THERETO; AND**
- 3. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 7404 WHICH ESTABLISHES THE 2004 CITY OF BOULDER PROPERTY TAX MILL LEVIES WHICH ARE TO BE COLLECTED BY THE COUNTY OF BOULDER, STATE OF COLORADO, WITHIN THE CITY OF BOULDER IN 2005 FOR PAYMENT OF EXPENDITURES BY THE CITY OF BOULDER, COUNTY OF BOULDER, STATE OF COLORADO, AND SETTING FORTH DETAILS IN RELATION THERETO; AND**
- 4. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 7405 WHICH APPROPRIATES MONEY TO DEFRAY EXPENSES AND LIABILITIES OF THE CITY OF BOULDER, COLORADO, FOR THE 2005 FISCAL YEAR OF THE CITY OF BOULDER, COMMENCING ON THE FIRST DAY OF JANUARY 2005, AND ENDING ON THE LAST DAY OF DECEMBER 2005, AND SETTING FORTH DETAILS IN RELATION THERETO; AND**
- 5. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 7406 WHICH AMENDS CHAPTERS 3-8, 3-9, 3-10, 3-11 AND 4-20, B.R.C. 1981, CHANGING CERTAIN FEES AND SETTING FORTH DETAILS IN RELATION THERETO.**

{1}------------------------------------------------

The public hearing was opened:

1. Tim Thomas, 2300 Arapahoe #327; re: Trash Tax – indicated that if the Trash Tax is increased the funds should go to restoring services such as library hours. Stated additional oversight needed for city purchasing.
2. Jann Scott, PO Box 869, 80306; re: CATV – spoke of the fundraising efforts currently planned to meet council expectations.
3. Lou Dellacava, 2418 30th Street, 80301; (pooled time with Violet Schmidt) representing Boulder Tomorrow – spoke to the numerous problems related to the Trash Tax increase – process, timing, equity, noting that during a time when the Boulder economy continues to decline is not appropriate.
4. Earl McLaughlin, 5541 Central Ave #145; President of Boulder Tomorrow – spoke to the investments of Xcel Energy to bring energy efficiency. Urged council to get better information.
5. Donald Wilson, PO Box 1046, 80306; re: Trash Tax – indicated that the trash tax is available to the general fund. It is a huge increase and its intended use adds to staffing levels at a time when departments are cutting staff and programs. It sends the wrong message.
6. Gale Dunlap, 1117 Barberry Ct., 80305; re: Trash Tax proposal – blatantly acts as a disincentive to conservation and recycling (see attachment).
7. Jane Jenkins, 1942 Broadway, Ste 301, 80302; Representing DBI – requesting that the Trash Tax be tabled until the community has opportunity to discuss and fully understand the issue.
8. Eric Johnson, 1111 Maxwell Ave #211, 80304; spoke in support of the tax increase noting that global warming is critical and must be addressed now. Encouraged residents and businesses to reduce waste.
9. Steve Clason, 3305 Eastman Ave, 80305; Library Commission Member – spoke to the library funding issue noting that while there is a savings from closing the library early, other negative impacts should be considered including the reduction of meeting space. Asked council to find the \$15,000.
10. Alan Streater, 2830 Dover Dr., 80305; supports trash tax – also suggested a head tax. Thanks for clearing bike paths.
11. Todd Runestad, 1828 Del Rosa Ct., 80304; re: Trash Tax – feels this is mission creep – original purpose not being maintained. Wrong time for a tax increase – felt it should be placed on a ballot.
12. Inta Morris, PO Box 270444, 80027; Rocky Mountain Climate Organization – speaking to the effects of climate change in Boulder. Less snow pack and less water will create major drought conditions. Emphasis on renewables and energy efficiencies needs to remain a priority.
13. Steve Pomerance, 335 17th St; supports energy efficiencies but does not feel this proposal is the right way to address that project. Concerned that far more over sight is needed.
14. Leslie Glustrom, 4492 Burr Place; spoke to the importance of addressing green house gas emissions.
15. Dan Powers, 2440 Pearl; Chamber of Commerce – requested that the Trash Tax increase be removed form consideration this evening to allow Business community to analyze the impacts.
16. Lisa Dilling, 830 33rd St., 80303; Scientist – in support of utilizing Trash Tax funds to address a critical problem.

{2}------------------------------------------------

17. Pam Milmoe, 3450 Broadway, 80305; in support of utilizing the trash tax to benefit the environment. Leadership in this area is critical.
18. Francoise Poinsatte, 2636 5th St., 80304; noted that when Boulder adopted the resolution in support of the Kyoto Protocol it was clear that addressing energy conservation would be key.
19. Ken Regelson, 1450 Riverside, 80304; Let's start acting locally – pass the Trash Tax.
20. Guy Errickson, 4705 Harrison Ave., 80303; supports public access TV, but noted that current program doesn't meet that definition.
21. Alison Burchell, PO Box 3671, 80305; re: trash tax - spoke to the importance of engaging in community dialogue. Indicated support of the program, but better outreach needed.
22. Bruce Bland, 1340 Lehigh St., 80305; Urged support of the Trash Tax – echoed that better communication needed, but felt it important to take this important step to address a critical problem.
23. Lisa Morzel, 2155 Poplar Ave, 80304; spoke to the importance of supporting the Trash Tax, noting that consumers have a choice – if they reduce waste the cost can be less. Encouraged approval.
24. Donna Marek, PO Box 7181, 80306; re: Channel 54 – indicated that the contract between the city and CATV is not valid. Indicated that mismanagement is evident and urged council to remove funding for CATV from the 2005 budget.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Toor moved, seconded by McGrath to adopt Ordinance No. 7403 which adopts a budget for the City of Boulder, Colorado, for the fiscal year commencing on the first day of January, 2005 and ending on the last day of December, 2005, and setting forth details in relation thereto. The motion carried 6:2; Riggle and Stoakes opposed, Schultheiss absent.

Councilmember Riggle moved, seconded by Stoakes to introduce an ordinance to rescind the Trash Tax component from the 2005 Budget. The motion failed 3:5; Bohannan, Gray, McGrath, Ruzzin and Toor opposed; Schultheiss absent.

Councilmember Gray moved, seconded by McGrath to adopt Ordinance No. 7404 which establishes the 2004 City of Boulder property tax mill levies which are to be collected by the County of Boulder, State of Colorado, within the City of Boulder in 2005 for payment of expenditures by the City of Boulder, County of Boulder, State of Colorado, and setting forth details in relation thereto. The motion carried 8:0; Schultheiss absent.

Councilmember Stoakes moved, seconded by Eldridge to adopt Ordinance No. 7405 which appropriates money to defray expenses and liabilities of the City of Boulder, Colorado, for the 2005 fiscal year of the City of Boulder, commencing on the first day of January 2005, and ending on the last day of December 2005, and setting forth details in relation thereto. The motion carried 7:1; Riggle opposed, Schultheiss absent.

Councilmember Eldridge moved, seconded by Gray to adopt **Ordinance No. 7406** which amends Chapters 3-8, 3-9, 3-10, 3-11 and 4-20, B.R.C. 1981, **changing certain fees** and setting forth details in relation thereto. The motion carried 6:2; Riggle and Stoakes opposed; Schultheiss absent.

{3}------------------------------------------------

Councilmember Gray moved, seconded by Stoakes to direct the City Manager to bring back funding options to restore \$15,000 to the library when the city has a clear idea of the revenue picture for 2004.

Councilmember Stoakes offered a friendly amendment to cut council meals as one option. The friendly amendment was not accepted.

Vote was taken on the motion to direct the City Manager to bring back funding options to restore \$15,000 to the library when the city has a clear idea of the revenue picture for 2004. The motion carried 6:2; Eldridge and Riggle opposed; Schultheiss absent.

Consideration of a motion to adjourn from City Council and convene as the Boulder Municipal Property Authority Board of Directors and;

The motion to adjourn from City Council and convene as the Boulder Municipal Property Authority Board of Directors was approved by acclamation.

### **F. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO SELECT A WATER RATE STRUCTURE. – 9:30P.M.**

The public hearing was opened:

1. Dan Powers, 2440 Pearl Street; Boulder Chamber of Commerce – supported staff alternative “Tailored.”
2. Peter Mayer, 1339 Hawthorn Ave; 80304; supported WRAB Water Budget proposal.
3. Jim Knoff, 320 Hollyberry Lane, 80305; WRAB member – voiced his support for the water budget approach.
4. Michael Brawner, 4795 26th, 80301; supported classic water budget proposal by WRAB.
5. Bill Deoro, 3030 15th St., 80304; spoke in support of the WRAB Water Budget proposal.
6. Charles Bergren, 6224 Old Brompton Rd., 80301; representing Gunbarrel Homeowner’s Association; they purchase water (2 million gallons) to water neighborhood park – supports WRAB “classic” approach.
7. Eric Johnson, 1111 Maxwell Ave #211, 80304; supports “classic” WRAB water budget proposal.
8. Steve Pomerance, 335 17th; supports water budget approach but indicated that pricing rate structure still needs to be addressed.
9. Amy Livingston, 3515 28th St. #201, 80301; Colorado Environmental Coalition; urged council to support the “classic” water budget approach proposed by the WRAB.
10. Francoise Poinsatte, 2636 5th St, 80304; provided some background from when she was on council. Indicated support for the classic water budget approach.
11. Hampton Islan, 335 Oneida, 80303; favors the budget rate structure.
12. Elise Jones, 716 Marine St, 80302; Colorado Environmental Coalition; spoke in support of the WRAB proposal for classic water budget approach.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Gray moved, seconded by McGrath to continue the Water Rate Structure item to the December 21, 2004 council meeting with a primary focus on WRAB discrepancy and cost estimates. The motion carried 6:0; Eldridge, Riggle and Schultheiss absent.
