### **B. DISCUSSION ABOUT THE DESIGN OF AN OUTREACH PROCESS FOR THE WASHINGTON SCHOOL SITE.**

Acting Planning Director Ruth McHeyser provided an overview of the objective for this discussion.

John Gless reported on behalf of the Washington School neighborhood that the group felt the process failed when the project was allowed to move forward with only one applicant in the bidding process. The neighborhood would like to see a process that would allow for review of more than one submittal. The public should also have options and an opportunity to indicate what they want or don't want. He suggested starting with a process that identifies what would be acceptable to the community.

{3}------------------------------------------------

Jim Leach and Jonathan Barbieri spoke from Wonderland Hill indicating 5 key elements which the process can use as a foundation. He asked that the charge from council be to establish a process that recognizes, respects, and incorporates the following:

- 1) The process should take into account the significant financial and time commitment made by the Washington Village cohousing members and Wonderland in reliance on the process to date that was set by the city and agreed upon by the city and the school district.
- 2) Wonderland does not want to start all over again and supports the idea of a process to inform a resubmittal of a site plan. They are willing to take the inspiration generated by the city council's vision when it released the RFQ in the fall of 2005, and with the collaboration of all stakeholders, have a site plan that is reflective of that vision, and includes a cohousing community, that is economically feasible and is reasonable for all.
- 3) The process needs to recognize that the School District's position is clear in that they are not willing to reopen a discussion on the closing of the school or a discounted site purchase price. Any public process on the appropriate use of surplus school district property needs to be independent of the public input process on the resubmitted Washington Village site plan.
- 4) The city's position was firm that it was not willing to subsidize the purchase of the site to enable greater public use of a portion of the site for a park or other use. He asked that this be verified or modified by the current council by January 22, 2008.
- 5) Wonderland intends to close on the site independent of its entitlement by April 8, 2008.

Mr. Leach indicated that it was critical that the city council be explicit about what the parameters are for the process. He voiced support for the ad hoc group to decide or recommend on the process for public input on the recent middle site plans with four requests:

- 1) The ad hoc group should also include representatives from the Washington Village co-housing community
- 2) The ad hoc group should be established and charged no later than January 22, 2008 and should begin meeting prior to the end of January.
- 3) The group should reach consensus on the process or bring its recommendations to the council no later than the second council meeting in February.
- 4) The public input process should be started no later than March 1 and completed no later than April 1, 2008.

He concluded his comments by reminding the Council that closing on the site is required by April 8, 2008.

Tim Plass from the Landmarks Preservation Advisory Board, speaking individually, noted that he was initially excited about this proposed process but having looked at positions from BVSD, the Washington School neighborhood, and Wonderland Hill fiscal constraints, he had difficulty in seeing a way to come up with a solution. He wished council luck in this process.

Elise Jones from the Planning Board spoke in support of an ad hoc group to explore options. However, council needs to clearly define parameters and scope to ensure a different outcome. She urged council to weigh in on the park option as this point could be critical in creating a solution.

{4}------------------------------------------------

Council deliberation began on possible options for moving forward.

After further discussion, staff was asked to bring back the following information for a public hearing on January 22:

1. Direction regarding an outreach process to occur prior to the submittal of a revised site review application to the city, and council direction on overall parameters regarding the appointment of a group to design the next steps in the process:
  - a. What the charge of the group should be;
  - b. Who should be on the group and involved in the design of the process; and
  - c. When the group should start and end and how the final decision on the design of the process should be made.Regarding parameters that would affect the extent of flexibility in site plan revisions:
  - a. Whether the city should consider funding a portion of the site for a park;
  - b. Whether the city should continue to require the community benefits stipulated in the earlier process or allow flexibility to remove some or all of the requirements in the covenant; and
  - c. Whether the pre-existing zoning (i.e., the zoning on the site in place prior to the referendum) should be the basis of the process moving forward.; and
2. Re-consideration of Ordinance No. 7559 and potential repeal of the rezoning of a portion of the site from low density residential to high density residential.

Deputy Mayor Gray moved, seconded by Morzel to suspend the rules and continue the meeting at 10:35 p.m. The motion carried 8:1; Espinoza opposed
