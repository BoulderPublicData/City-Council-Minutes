# **City Council Study Session May 19, 2008 East Boulder Senior Center 5660 Sioux Drive**

## **Council's Six Month Review**

## **Agenda:**

|                                                                       |                          |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| <b>1) Comments from Council members</b>                               | <b>6:30 pm - 7:15 pm</b> |
| <b>2) Comments from City Attorney and City Manager</b>                | <b>7:15 pm – 7:30 pm</b> |
| <b>3) Discussion &amp; proposals to adjust goals and/or processes</b> | <b>7:30 pm – 8:30 pm</b> |
| <b>4) Next steps (if needed)</b>                                      | <b>8:30 pm – 8:45 pm</b> |

The study session convened at 6:30 p.m.

Those present were: Mayor McGrath, Deputy Mayor Gray, and Council Members Ageton, Appelbaum, Cowles, Espinoza, Osborne, and Wilson.

Council Member Morzel was absent.

Council Member Ageton briefly reviewed the agenda for the meeting. Council decided to discuss each topic individually.

Additional items for discussion were raised including council representation at events and a discussion about “what’s been going well” versus “what hasn’t been going well.”

## **Progress on council goals:**

Council Member Wilson acknowledged some discouraged feelings of council members about what’s been accomplished. He noted that staff doesn’t work for council individually or directly and that he’s not surprised that goals are taking a long time.

Deputy Mayor Gray referenced Council Member Cowles’ Business Improvement District (BID) list and recommended that council periodically review a list of accomplishments (even smaller items) as a reminder of council’s achievements. Examples are the committee to set up the city manager search and the committee appointing an interim city manager. She hopes that the city-wide quarterly newsletter will allow for council to communicate accomplishments to the public.

{1}------------------------------------------------

Mayor McGrath spoke of the goals discussed at the January retreat acknowledging that there are only two years for this council to complete the goals. The key is to stay focused on goals and balance those with new issues raised by individual members. He suggested tracking the goals, perhaps by reviewing what is on the goals list before accepting other projects.

Council Member Cowles voiced that he doesn't feel discouraged with council's progress but he feels worn out. As a new member he quickly realized there is other important city business that ties up a lot of council time. He suggested council members be forceful with pushing important items while being steadfast with mundane business. He is proud of how the council has worked together. He acknowledged that pops and scrapes could have been approached better without causing so much disruption. He felt it ended well in that the community was engaged and it focused the attention of the city and residents on the problem which will hopefully result in a good solution. What he finds most satisfying is how the council is working together. He asked for feedback about his behavior at council meetings.

Council Member Cowles provided feedback to Council Member Appelbaum that at times he takes too much time in walking through his analysis of issues when posing a question or concern. However, he did agree that council should take the time necessary as long as they are prepared. He would like to know more about where each person's expertise lies to better rely on the experience on council.

Council Member Ageton reflected that there are learning opportunities from the pops and scrapes issue. Council members were way out in front of the community, particularly in timing and the community responded negatively. She feels it is important to work on relationship building with other entities and the general community. It seemed a proposal for an outcome was being brought forward that wasn't sufficiently vetted. This is a key learning opportunity. Council didn't bring it to the table in a way that brought the community along. A new idea was introduced to the public on a Saturday and discussed at a Tuesday meeting and much more notice should be provided to the public.

Council Member Osborne asked her colleagues how this could have been handled better.

Council Member Ageton noted that she doesn't feel council allowed sufficient time and notice but asked the question - how much notice do we provide to be better functioning? An action was proposed that would have taken immediate effect and this caught people off guard, including staff.

Council Member Osborne reminded the group that council had met with Planning Board and discussed this topic.

Deputy Mayor Gray raised concern that there was misinformation circulating in the public. The challenge is to correct the inaccurate information. She suggested letters to the editor as a way to manage information.

{2}------------------------------------------------

Council Member Wilson suggested that it may be better to ask Planning Board to come back with options about how to address these types of issues. The information in the community may not have been incorrect but the interpretation was and it looked like council could stop all building if it chose to.

Council Member Ageton pointed out the response from the public was not unexpected. This issue touched many personal and private assets. It is a lesson about being thoughtful about how issues are introduced.

City Manager Bruno acknowledged the balancing act council must perform and noted it's important to talk to the public about what's going well. He used the example of how the Capital Improvements Program (CIP) becomes this council's budget by diving in at study sessions. He noted the importance of leaving room for other important items as well as sharpening the focus on council goals. He also noted that Council Matters are taking up extensive time at meetings. Better scheduling is needed for council matters. He indicated that this council needs to get focused on its priorities as the lack of focus compromises council's ability to achieve success. He also acknowledged that council never concluded with a top five list from the retreat but instead ended with a long list.

Regarding pops and scrapes, he raised the question – who speaks for council? There are nine individual view points and the *Daily Camera* doesn't care if it's one council member or all. An agreement is needed about who speaks for council or it will be important to clarify that you're not speaking on behalf of council but as an individual. He asked council to consider what it wants to succeed at.

Mayor McGrath felt the process was consistent with procedure but putting something on the agenda seemed rushed. He indicated that council should be more thoughtful about scheduling and consider appearances. This moved the issue further along than what otherwise might have occurred although that wasn't the objective. He acknowledged that issues don't start with consensus (among council or the public) and council should expect this and allow the process to take place.

Council Member Espinoza noted that regardless of timing and process there is always going to be misinformation floating in community. Council should have a better press strategy to get the message out. She asked her colleagues - What is our message as a legislative body and how do we get the message out more effectively? Council does not have a strong enough voice in the public.

Council Member Osborne agreed and indicated that she is still not understanding how the pops and scrapes issue could have been managed differently. Maybe someone on the city manager's staff can help the council strategize.

Deputy Mayor Gray said she appreciated knowing council members' positions and having those out ahead of time. It is better than after public participation because the public can't contribute. She suggested using the hotline to give a heads up about positions. She also suggested consolidating some of the pages in the reference section of

{3}------------------------------------------------

the agendas and adding the council goals document to the packet. She reiterated the importance of communicating good things to the public such as consent items approved, property purchases, housing, arts, and Youth Opportunities Advisory Board (YOAB) grants. She also applauded work on the quarterly newsletter as this may help serve this purpose by focusing on accomplishments that are positive for the community.

City Attorney Jerry Gordon, speaking as an observer rather than in his legal role, noted his impression that this council hasn't yet quite hit its stride. He recalled that when council was first discussing its goals for its first 100 days, it occurred to him that a longer time frame of reference might be useful with regard to some issues. He also suggested that council might want to consider focusing on a limited number of priorities in order to be most effective.

Regarding effectiveness at meetings, he noted council's experience with the pops and scrapes issue might be instructive. He suggested that one reason that the conversation may have become difficult was that the original public from which council heard was an "inside the beltway" group and not the more general "outside the beltway" public of the larger community. He also suggested that council may have inadvertently projected a crisis atmosphere that contributed to a push back response from some members of the public.

Finally, he suggested that council members may wish to consider whether their council meeting comments are as succinct as possible. He noted that masters in various fields are often notable for the efficiency and apparent ease with which they practice their craft. He suggested that council members might ask themselves whether, when they begin to speak, they are clear about the final destination point for their comments.

Council Member Appelbaum felt Mayor McGrath hit the mark on the pops and scrapes issue and councils generally don't try to do things that quickly. He said he felt there was pressure to deal with the issue quickly but that is not the right approach. Also, he noted that it is unusual for an issue to affect such a large portion of the population. Sometimes a longer process may still take the public off guard ending up in the same situation. He pointed out that you can't stop individuals from communicating with the press and there is no control over what the papers pick up on. He said this council still needs a better way to synthesize an agreement about what the entire council is directing, approving etc. and he would look to the Mayor for help at council meetings.

City Manager Bruno mentioned that it would be difficult for staff to manage the press because it sometimes doesn't know where the full council will ultimately land on an issue. It may be best for staff to callout serious inaccuracies. Council should be able to voice opinions but need to be very clear if you are speaking as an individual. He felt a disconnect with this issue and what the city process should be. He pointed out that the public may become offended if council jumps to a solution without consulting the people who voted for them. He reflected on a situation where it appeared that council pulled a report because of three public speakers in opposition when the report truly had been well worked and was ready to move forward.

{4}------------------------------------------------

Council Member Espinoza voiced that she doesn't feel there are currently council goals, only categories of goals. She never felt consensus was reached on the goals on which this council could work together. She would like to see more focus on topics the council can achieve together.

Council Member Cowles suggested that more detailed council minutes would be helpful noting that it is impossible to remember what each person says on a topic of interest. It is important to have a reference. Council would do a much better job if minutes reflected what people actually said.

Council Member Appelbaum noted that with more detailed minutes the information would be available but the final outcome or answer may still not be clear. It may still be necessary to go back and assess the issues.

Mayor McGrath suggested caution in believing straw polls done by the media or the public in that it pushes council into an outcome before discussion. He agreed with Council Member Appelbaum that discussions need to be more synthesized. He suggested more focus in study sessions to reflect on only a few issues at once.

Council Member Osborne suggested dot voting or something on hotline to narrow the goals. She also suggested assessing whether a project is working financially before proceeding and looking again at the goals in order to focus on priorities.

Deputy Mayor Gray agreed with Council Member Osborne's comments and suggested council members watch council meetings rebroadcast on Channel 8 or online. It is helpful for perspective and to recapture comments that quickly go by during meetings. She also agreed that council should rely on the experience each member brings to the table.

Council Member Ageton raised the importance of considering the purpose in speaking with the press and that council members think more carefully about what is said publicly. If you're the only response – your view becomes prominent. She also suggested council consider the impact on the city organization and its partners. The hotline serves the same purpose (as a public venue). She then noted that 5-4 votes is a split council and this is not the goal to which council should aspire. Council should seek to work more as a team rather than individuals.

## **Conduct and effectiveness of Council meetings and study sessions:**

Council Member Wilson indicated that council needs to decide how much comment council members will make in response after public participation. He noted that council members' questions need to be more disciplined and the question period should be kept separate from the comment period. He also raised concern about time allocation per member noting that sometimes members speak more if they are experienced in particular issues. He also raised concern that some council members speak too long on every issue.

{5}------------------------------------------------

He indicated he would support the Mayor in more management of the “stack” so that each member of council has the opportunity to speak once before another member speaks twice. He suggested members express their main points, keep questions brief, and support the Mayor in more strictly managing or cutting off if someone goes too long. This will help address the meeting adjournment time.

Deputy Mayor Gray raised concern that questions come only at the end of a staff presentation. She noted that longer presentations should allow more opportunity for questions throughout.

Council Member Wilson suggested taking notes to remember questions.

Deputy Mayor Gray suggested making motions earlier so that conversations are more directed.

Council Member Ageton suggested asking if there is anyone who hasn’t spoken.

Mayor McGrath acknowledged the difficult situation it places the Mayor in and that this also requires a lot of self regulation.

Council Member Cowles mentioned a technique that worked on Planning Board which involved setting aside an hour to talk about a topic, the Chair reminded everyone at 20-minute and 30-minute intervals to keep the conversation moving forward.

There was general consensus indicated to support the Mayor in stepping in and moving conversations forward.

Council Member Espinoza acknowledged that Mayor McGrath does well to keep things on track and suggested including in the minutes the length of time each council member spoke and a short summary of their comments.

Council Member Appelbaum noted that sometimes having all members speak to an issue is helpful in knowing what issues are supported or not. If only one person speaks it is difficult to know how this is interpreted by staff, particularly when no specific council action is taken by motion.

The City Manager noted that staff generally does not move forward with direction by an individual council member which is why summaries become even more important.

City Attorney Gordon suggested that council consider taking more votes than it has in the recent past - particularly when staff direction is involved. That way staff can receive clearer guidance about exactly what the council (rather than an individual council member) expects. He offered to come up with some procedural options for study sessions in this regard. He also suggested that council members attempt to be very clear when stating a position for which they are seeking support in order to avoid any confusion after the fact about a council decision or direction.

{6}------------------------------------------------

Council generally concurred that the City Attorney should research procedural and voting options for study sessions.

## **Council Agenda Committee (CAC) – its function and effectiveness:**

Mayor McGrath acknowledged some misunderstanding by new members that the CAC does not discuss policy. The meeting should be more about agenda materials, whether the issue is ready to move forward, and to help frame conversations.

The City Manager noted that the city manager is charged with bringing CAC the agenda. This is a collaborative process between the city manager, city clerk and city attorney.

Deputy Mayor Gray noted that more frequent rotations are helpful and will assist new members in keeping in touch with the issues and processes. She offered her seat on the CAC rotation for other council members if they are interested.

Council Member Osborne asked staff to list the times for each item on all of the agendas that are placed on the council dais.

Council Member Ageton raised concern about when an agenda is considered final acknowledging that recently the agenda has been changing after the final has been sent out and it is confusing. Only true emergency items should be added.

City Manager Bruno indicated that the leadership team creating the agenda (CAC) should be as consistent as possible.

Council Member Ageton noted that there should be a larger expectation that short of an emergency, things don't get dropped in or pulled at the last minute. Council and staff should seek more adequate notice.

Council Member Cowles noted that pulling and adding items, in each case, is a very different situation and often includes additional last minute information from staff.

Deputy Mayor Gray acknowledged that, as a council member, she felt out of the loop without attending CAC. That's why she's volunteering her CAC spot occasionally. It is important to have an engaged council. She felt CAC could weigh in more heavily rather than treating CAC issues as first reading questions. She suggested under council matters that CAC watch the number of items and how long each takes and asked for direction about what to do with a matter that is going to be a longer discussion.

City Attorney Gordon suggested that it can be dangerous when matters become ad hoc discussions.

### **Interaction with city staff:**

Deputy Mayor Gray asked the City Manager how he would like council to communicate with staff.

The City Manager responded that there seems to be an awful lot of reaching into the organization at different levels. When council poses questions to staff at a director level then council begins directing staff. Council should try to contact directors. When the manager is not informed, he/she is not able to properly manage the organization in terms of work load. He encouraged council to re-commit to contacting staff at the director level and that anything beyond a quick question should be directed to the city manager.

Deputy Mayor Gray asked about copying the city manager on interactions with staff. The city manager responded that copying the city manager is appropriate when questions require more extensive research.

City Attorney Gordon read section 13 of the city Charter which, in summary, states that,

"Except for purposes of inquiry, the council shall deal with the administrative service solely and directly through the city manager, and neither council, its members, nor committees shall either dictate the appointment, retention or removal or direct or interfere with the work of any officer or employee under the city manager..."

He explained the difficult position into which staff members can be placed when a request is received directly from a council member.

Council Member Osborne raised concern that she feels it is often like trying to do the job with one hand tied behind her back. There are too many rules which make it very difficult.

City Manager Bruno relayed the importance of developing a good rapport with staff without pressuring or pushing staff towards a particular agenda.

### **Interactions with the community; public and private partners:**

Council Member Ageton suggested that council members should recognize when working on projects that there are other community partners to consider. For example, there have been struggles in the past with Boulder Valley School District (BVSD) over a number of topics. There are many important relationships to consider.

Council Member Appelbaum noted the many interactions with external groups (officially appointed or not). When one is not appointed, a member can't go talk on behalf of council. It is more of a problem when two members do it and create the impression that it's the opinion of the entire council. When a member is officially appointed it is

{8}------------------------------------------------

important to be very careful and keep distance/skepticism. Don't become the champion of the cause - we all have the job to represent the full council.

Council Member Wilson disagreed with Council Member Appelbaum noting that this wastes potential. It is important to be a part of the community and interact.

Mayor McGrath suggested individual members let council know before hand when something is coming up and that individual members make a clear statement as to whether they are speaking on behalf of council, a committee, or themselves.

Council Member Osborne felt that a signature on a petition such as council member Morzel signing in support of separating school zones is tricky. We are members of the community in spite of being on council.

Council Member Appelbaum suggested that some groups expect council representatives to be advocates.

Council Member Cowles spoke regarding BMOCA noting that the advocate role is sometimes presumed.

Mayor McGrath noted that the other side is that council's interests are represented and sometimes things must be brought back to council as the conduit.

Council Member Gray suggested that city sponsored events should include the full council in an invitation.

### **Communications (e.g. hotline, media interactions etc...):**

The issue of hotline was raised as a reminder that no serial discussions should take place, only statements to council members or questions to staff.

Council Member Appelbaum feels that the hotline isn't working well.

Council Member Espinoza noted that she uses hotline to bring an issue forward or when she wants to ask CAC to schedule something or give a heads up on a controversial item. She acknowledged that the hotline is a limited tool. She noted the importance of being mindful about working; don't write a hotline that provokes a response, just put an issue forward to be discussed at later time.

Council Member Ageton noted that it's important to be sensitive to the role staff plays in many projects and keep them in the loop and informed about what's going on. Staff appreciates hearing information prior to it coming up. Please share information and exchange ideas with staff.

City Attorney Gordon noted that council is doing a reasonably good job with hotline but that the use of hotline always presents difficult issues. He also noted that under some

{9}------------------------------------------------

circumstances, the use of hotline may not be the most effective forum for communication. He suggested that a specific question to the city attorney is often best handled with a phone call or direct e-mail. It can be awkward for an attorney to provide legal advice on the hotline.

### **What's going well, what's not going well?:**

Council Member Osborne noted that one surprise of what worked well was Washington school. It has turned out to be a good surprise after the many months of deliberation and she believes the community felt heard. This issue may conclude positively.

City Attorney Gordon said he was proud of council's conversation on Valmont Butte. The discussion went very well and he thought there was noticeable growth among the group.

Council Member Appelbaum felt council did well with the pops and scrapes questions and concerns.

Council Member Espinoza feels this council is culturally competent. She feels like she can talk about things in this group that she wouldn't be as confident discussing in other settings.

Mayor McGrath thought the Xcel discussion on municipalization, *Smart Grid*, and the franchise agreement went well. Not only was the outcome positive but it was also handled well. This has already had positive impacts in negotiations.

Council Member Cowles mentioned the absence of detail in the meeting minutes.

Deputy Mayor Gray noted that more emphasis should be placed in the human services area to highlight the incredibly important, good work and partnerships with non-profits. There should be more emphasis on core values, again, possibly highlighted in the quarterly newsletter.

Council Member Ageton acknowledged the difficulty in setting priorities at the retreat. The ability to move forward is dependent upon consensus among council. She expressed that it is critical to have consensus on top priorities for the next two years.

Council Member Cowles noted that council is in a better position to choose priorities as a group than it was in January, after having submitting lists and working together more closely. He agreed with Deputy Mayor Gray about emphasizing the human services work. He feels CAC needs more flexibility with the agenda for presentations. 6 – 6:30 p.m. is the most valuable time for items such as what's going on at town hall or other special presentations.
