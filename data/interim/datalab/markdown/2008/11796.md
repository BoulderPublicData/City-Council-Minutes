### **City Manager Response - 6:54 p.m.**

Interim City Manager Grainger responded as follows:

- 1. Regarding Orchard Grove, she provided a brief update that council last heard this item on June 3 and directed staff to explore options that included rezoning the park to mobile home zoning (MH-Z); conducting a dialogue with residents and the buyer regarding potential redevelopment of the site; and a potential city subsidy or a combination of these options. ### **A. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION RECOMMENDING THE VALMONT CITY PARK FINAL CONCEPT PLAN AND THE DEVELOPMENT OF PHASING STRATEGIES MOVE FORWARD TO SITE REVIEW. – 7:06 P.M.**

Presenters Jan Geden and Alice Guthrie from Parks and Recreation provided a brief overview of the history of Valmont Park noting that a ballot issue in 1995 provided funding to purchase the park for recreational uses. In 2006 the Parks and Recreation Master Plan was approved, and at that time council requested an updated concept plan for the Valmont Park. Alice Guthrie reviewed the final park concept plan with clarification regarding the amphitheater location proposal indicating that disc golf was not a compatible use with the amphitheater.

Council Member Morzel asked for clarification around Planning Board’s concern about the transportation component of the plan. Planning Board Chair Phil Shull responded that the Planning Board felt there was a high level of pedestrian access to the park that should be incentivized. The Planning Board also discouraged excessive parking at the site for that reason. There was concern relative to the amount (or lack of) passive recreational space at the park. Council Member Morzel asked whether charging for parking was discussed. Mr. Shull clarified that this was not discussed.

{4}------------------------------------------------

Council Member Osborne asked if it was anticipated that the underpass in Phase II would be funded by Transportation or Parks and Recreation. Staff indicated that discussions between Transportation and Parks and Recreation are underway regarding joint funding. Planning Board Chair Phil Shull noted that the Planning Board also discussed ensuring that the bike connection is strong and safe.

Deputy Mayor Gray asked about availability of shaded structures for picnics. Staff clarified there would be plenty of picnic areas.

Council Member Wilson asked what was lost in the relocation of the amphitheater. Ms. Guthrie clarified that it is anticipated that staff will find a way to incorporate both uses at the site. He also asked about ongoing operating costs for each phase. Ms. Guthrie indicated that staff hasn't flushed out the operating costs yet. General costs for turf are \$3,000-\$5,000 an acre. Staff has never operated a bike park before so that number is currently unknown. Staff is in the process of developing an agreement with the Boulder Mountain Bike Alliance to assist with maintenance of the park. Ms. Geden noted the interest from the cycling community in helping the city to maintain the bike park.

Council Member Appelbaum clarified that many of the issues around the amphitheater and disc golf locations would or could be addressed during Phase II and many options would be available. Staff confirmed. He also asked how much land would actually be available for disc golf. Ms. Guthrie clarified that the layout is the most important consideration in terms of safety for residents using other areas of the park.

Council Member Ageton asked about prairie dog removal and barriers and whether this is all included in the \$120,000 budget. Staff responded that this was included but that monitoring is not included in the \$120,000. Ms. Ageton also asked about timing to begin Phase II. Ms. Geden indicated that there is a \$15 million price tag associated with Phase II and no definite plans about when that phase would begin.

Mayor McGrath asked Planning Chair Phil Shull about the south side of the park and raised concerns about whether the Planning Board discussed the intensity of use throughout phase II. He also asked whether Planning Board considered the unmet needs in Boulder's park system and that the open space program has unrivaled passive recreation compared to other communities. Mr. Shull noted that this was discussed, but Planning Board's concern was whether this park could be multiple things to multiple people; not just all about sports.

{5}------------------------------------------------

Council Member Morzel asked about the pedestrian underpass and whether there were two planned for the area. Staff confirmed there is one existing at Wonderland Creek with the potential for one planned in another location. Ms. Morzel asked how much it would cost to include the underpass in Phase I. Ms. Guthrie responded that she did not have the exact number but it would be over \$1 million from Park funds. Council Member Morzel also commented that she hoped port-o-lets are not going to be used in the Park but rather some type of compostable toilets. She noted that in the past it was discussed to dedicate part of the park to a wildlife corridor and incorporate prairie dogs as a passive and educational component. Ms. Guthrie indicated that this was not a part of the current plan, however there are still easements along the north side populated with cottonwood trees that would be used by wildlife and the city will work with the ditch companies to maintain this open area. The Wonderland Creek corridor is also a nice wetland area for wildlife. Ms. Morzel also asked why the two commercial areas aren't consolidated in the same location. Ms. Guthrie replied that there is a private property parcel in the area which separates the two commercial developments.

Council Member Appelbaum asked whether the Phase I funding is currently available. Ms. Guthrie noted that the \$4.5 million figure includes \$3.9 million from CIP funding and the \$4.5 million figure has a large contingency for design and green building that staff anticipates coming in overtime.

Jan Geden recognized that Parks and Recreation Advisory Board members Norm Murphy and Pete Webber were also present in the audience.

#### **Ordinance No 7599 – Council Compensation**

Council Member Appelbaum moved, seconded by Wilson to introduce and order published by title only, Ordinance No. 7599 placing the question of council compensation on the November 4, 2008 ballot.

##### **Discussion on the motion:**

Council Member Appelbaum commented that this is an incredibly hard working council and that the ballot measure last year failed by a small margin. He expressed that council salary should reflect the amount of work that goes into doing the job of a legislative body.

#### **Ordinance No. 7600 – Executive Sessions**

Council Member Ageton moved, seconded by Appelbaum to introduce and order published by title only Ordinance No. 7600 placing the question of allowing council to convene executive sessions on the November 4, 2008 ballot with an amendment on page 12, under Section 9: "Meetings of council," subsection (a), deleting the word "future," prior to the word "purchase."

{10}------------------------------------------------

##### **Discussion on the motion:**

Council Member Ageton expressed that good governance cannot happen without addressing certain issues in executive session. She noted that in order to discuss some confidential or legal matters, council discusses these issues in groups of two when unable to discuss it publicly. This does not serve as an efficient use of time for council, staff, or, in some cases, outside counsel, who is paid by the hour. She also expressed that this is consistent with the Colorado Open Meetings Act.

Council Member Appelbaum agreed with Council Member Ageton's comments noting that this would make things more, rather than less open. The public will be aware of the executive session and topic being discussed, whereas private meetings are not open and the public is not aware of them. He suggested requiring a two-thirds or three-quarters vote of council to go into executive session.

Council Member Morzel echoed the previous speakers' comments. She spoke to her history on council and expressed the inefficiency in dealing with legal matters without the ability to discuss matters with all of her colleagues. She also spoke to the excessive costs incurred from having to spend more time in smaller discussions. In addition, she noted that over 60% of neighboring communities have the ability to meet in executive sessions. She supported the motion.

Deputy Mayor Gray noted that she would not support this item due to the history and practice of open government in Boulder and believes the perception created with the residents is that council is doing things behind closed doors.

Council Member Espinoza indicated support of this concept.

Council Member Cowles expressed that executive sessions would help in negotiating salaries with council's employees and would support the motion.

Council Member Wilson agreed with his colleagues comments in support of this item.

Mayor McGrath also indicated support of the item moving forward and also supported consideration of the 2/3 vote of members present before going into executive session.

Council Member Ageton asked for the City Attorney's opinion about the two-third vote issue.

City Attorney Gordon noted that the real issue is whether residents would prefer the two-third requirement. He also indicated that the item could move forward with a requirement of a two-thirds vote of all council members or a two-thirds vote of members present at a particular meeting. He also noted that the compensation item could be amended on first or second reading.

Council Member Appelbaum offered a friendly amendment to require a two-third vote of the quorum present to convene an executive session. The friendly amendment was accepted.

{11}------------------------------------------------

Vote was taken on the motion to introduce and order published by title only Ordinance No. 7600 placing the question of allowing council to convene executive sessions on the November 4, 2008 ballot with an amendment on page 12, under Section 9: "Meetings of council," subsection (a), deleting the word "future," prior to the word "purchase" and to require a two thirds vote of the quorum present to convene an executive session. The motion carried 8:1; Gray opposed.

Council Member Ageton asked the City Attorney to comment on the issues raised by Mr. Golden relating to the ACLU's opposition to non-electors serving on city boards and commissions.

City Attorney Gordon indicated that if someone made an allegation that that there had been a violation of the city charter, we would have an obligation to investigate the allegation. If this was a concern for council, another option would be to re-write the item to require residency in the city for a year and be 18 years of age and say nothing about legality. He expressed general agreement with Mr. Golden's comments.

Deputy Mayor Gray asked about consistency in referring to commissions vs. advisory commissions and/or boards in the charter language.

{14}------------------------------------------------

City Attorney Gordon noted that this could be cleaned up.

Council Member Espinoza asked whether there are other situations where local governments might be required to determine citizenship status related to municipal programs.

City Attorney Gordon noted that there are requirements that contractors participate in certain programs and a requirement that new employees attest that they are citizens. These do exist in law enforcement to comply with the law. He clarified that the city is required to do this in cases where it's required by state law but there is currently nothing in the city charter requiring that the city ask someone about their local citizenship status.

Council Member Appelbaum spoke to his motion noting that he would like to see this move forward to the ballot and that it would prompt a much needed community conversation. He indicated a desire to leave the legal resident wording in but would support removing that language if that's what the council supported.

Council Member Osborne looked at the Boulder County requirements for their boards and commissions noting that they don't ask residents about age or citizenship but only for an individual's address within the county and occupation. She suggested the city is making it more difficult rather than easier to attract diversity on boards and commissions.

Mayor McGrath supported diversity on boards but raised concern that focusing on legal citizenship becomes divisive. He indicated he would support a motion to change residency requirements if it steered away from the citizenship debate.

Council Member Appelbaum confirmed that what council would be considering is changing the requirements for boards and commissions service to only require one year of residency in Boulder.

Deputy Mayor Gray offered a substitute motion, seconded by McGrath to introduce and order published by title only Ordinance No. 7605 (option 1) as drafted in attachment G but to only require residency of one year.

##### **Discussion on the substitute motion:**

Council Member Wilson raised concern that this discussion was prompted by what Boulder County does and wanted to raise awareness that the County's boards don't have some of the powers that some of the city's boards have.

{15}------------------------------------------------

Council Member Ageton agreed with Council Member Wilson and raised the issue of board type and authority. She suggested reconsidering option II and noted that some boards have delegation of authority from council to make final decisions. The motion under consideration doesn't take into account the different responsibilities of the boards.

Council Member Morzel echoed Council Member Ageton's concerns. She would like to see option II with the word legal removed.

Council Member Appelbaum indicated support of the original motion or the substitute motion. He commented that board and commission members are dealing with local issues therefore he supports only the residency requirement. He also noted the thorough interview process, which helps screen qualified board members.

Council Member Espinoza expressed that she did not think a board or commission's authority or powers should have any bearing on qualifications. She supported the substitute motion as stated.

Council Member Osborne commented on the importance of considering all aspects of an applicant's qualifications.

Council Member Ageton raised concern about whether residents would support this issue on the ballot.

Council Member Cowles supported the substitute motion and felt the residents would find the residency requirement fair.

Council Member Appelbaum suggested approving the wording on first reading so that the item doesn't have to come back for a third reading.

City Attorney Gordon indicated that the ordinance language could be amended in attachment G, page 35, section 130, to remove the word "electors" and replace it with the word "members," and at the end add the phrase, "and who have resided in the city of Boulder for at least one year immediately prior to appointment to serve on the commission. This was accepted as a friendly amendment.

Vote was taken on the substitute motion to introduce and order published by title only Ordinance No. 7605 (option 1) as amended in attachment G, page 35, section 130, to remove the word "electors" and replace it with the word "members," and at the end add the phrase, "and who have resided in the city of Boulder for at least one year immediately prior to appointment to serve on the commission.. The motion carried 7:2; Ageton and Morzel opposed.
