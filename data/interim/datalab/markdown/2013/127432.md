#### **A. PROPOSED ORDINANCES IMPLEMENTING AMENDMENT 64 TO THE COLORADO STATE CONSTITUTION.**

1. **INTRODUCTION, FIRST READING AND CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ORDER PUBLISHED BY TITLE ONLY AN ORDINANCE AMENDING SECTION 6-14-1, B.R.C. 1981, AMENDING SECTION 6-14-2, B.R.C. 1981, AMENDING SECTION 6-14-5, B.R.C. 1981, AMENDING SECTION 6-14-7, B.R.C. 1981, AMENDING SECTION 6-14-8, B.R.C. 1981, AND AMENDING SECTION 6-14-13, B.R.C. 1981 ALL TO IMPLEMENT AND COMPLY WITH THE REQUIREMENTS OF AMENDMENT 64 TO THE COLORADO STATE CONSTITUTION AND SETTING FORTH RELATED DETAILS**
2. **INTRODUCTION, FIRST READING AND CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ORDER PUBLISHED BY TITLE ONLY AN ORDINANCE ADDING A NEW CHAPTER 5-10, B.R.C. 1981 TO IMPLEMENT AND COMPLY WITH THE REQUIREMENTS OF AMENDMENT 64 TO THE COLORADO STATE CONSTITUTION AND SETTING FORTH RELATED DETAILS**

Mayor Appelbaum introduced Christian Sederberg, both a drafter of Amendment 64 as well as a member of the State Amendment 64 Task Force. He recounted his experience and role in the drafting of Amendment 64 and his work with the state level working groups on implementation of Amendment 64.

Tom Carr, City Attorney, and Kathy Hadlock, Senior Assistant Attorney, gave the presentation reviewing the highlights of the three proposed ordinances.

Council Member Wilson expressed concern about the increased availability of marijuana to those under the age of twenty-one. He asked what thoughts came out of the working groups.

Mr. Sederberg responded that any left marijuana was readily available to people of all ages, by legalizing marijuana and regulating it the hope was to drive down the underground market. Businesses that have an interest in protecting their license to operate would follow the regulations around identification and age requirements.

Council Member Wilson noted that a letter had been received from the Boulder County Health Department expressing concerns about children and their safety in relation to marijuana legalization.

Council Member Tava asked for a map showing the proposed zoning for recreational marijuana with a layer showing the 1,000 foot rule.

Council Member Ageton asked to see these costs that are known and to have a discussion about how indirect costs might be covered on an ongoing basis. She then asked if anyone knew how the state of Washington was working through these issues.

Senior Assistant Attorney Haddock responded that Washington had hired private counsel to work on the matter. Washington was planning to regulate recreational marijuana at the state level, she noted that Colorado has many more home-rule municipalities than Washington.

Mr. Sederberg commented that Washington was planning to use their liquor board as the licensing authority.

Council Member Karakehian asked if there was going to be anywhere outside of private residences that would allow smoking. His concern was an increase in public consumption of marijuana.

Council Member Morzel noted that the smoking ban prevents smoking in public and businesses, but there are options for edibles and other delivery mechanisms.

Council Member Wilson asked how hookah stores were regulated.

City Attorney Carr responded that he was not sure his understanding was that they could sell paraphernalia but not allow smoking inside the business. He hypothesized that it may be viewed as burning rather than smoking.

Council Member Glass quoted a section of the ordinance related to offenses and noted that it makes it illegal to possess marijuana in a public place or a place viewable by the public. He was concerned that wasn't taking into account transporting the purchased good from the business to one's home.

Senior Assistant Attorney Haddock responded that an exception may need to be included, but the customer could place the product in their pocket and transport it out of view.

Council Member Cowles asked why the ordinance required stores to be located a minimum of 500 feet from high schools, middle schools and elementary schools and yet requires the same stores to be located 1,000 feet from the university.

City Attorney Carr responded that the 1,000 foot rule follows federal guidelines, where the 500 foot rule was adopted by Council. If Boulder were to have less restrictive licensing rules in place, the federal government could have the authority to shut down those businesses that do not meet the requirements under federal law.

Council Member Cowles asked for clarification on square footage of businesses and number of plants allowed for grow operations.

City Attorney Carr explained that the lower square footage for the recreational marijuana business takes into account the requirement for the medical business to offer some sort of wellness area or caregiver operation that would not be required in a recreational facility. The limitation on plants for grow operations was based on how many plants an officer could reasonably inspect.

Council Member Agerton commented that the questions being asked by staff need to be addressed before they could have a substantive discussion about any changes that should be made to the ordinances being presented.

Council Member Cowles asked why it was so important to get the ordinances passed if there was a chance the council might decide to put a moratorium in place until the middle of 2014.

City Attorney Carr commented that two of the three were required by Amendment 64 to determine the medical marijuana. Council Member Cowles pointed out that following the medical marijuana model may not be the best route and he would like to see more public process around the topic. He expressed concern about honoring the purpose of Amendment 64 and what the citizenry voted for, specifically when and where marijuana is available and legal to use. He pointed out that he is in favor of a longer moratorium that would extend through 2014 while answers to staff questions are able to go through the public process.

Council Member Morzel reminded Council that there was very strong support for Amendment 64 and its passing. She noted the importance of honoring the public's wishes.

Council Member Karakchian advised that he was also in favor of a moratorium as well.

Council Member Flase stressed that the voters spoke clearly and he would hesitantly support a shorter moratorium. He expressed the desire to pass the ordinances on first reading and take a proactive approach that other communities might use as a model.

Council Member Becker stated that she would like to see a tax implemented concurrently with a licensing framework and the opportunity to pass a tax occurs in November, leaving a short time frame for decision-making.

### **3. DISCUSSION OF REDUCED RECREATION CENTER FEES FOR LOW INCOME CHILDREN AND YOUTH WHO RESIDE IN THE ENCLAVES. - 1:40 PM**

Council Member Becker presented on this item; she requested reduced recreation center fees for low-income children and youth that attend Boulder Valley School located within the city of Boulder.

Council Member Ageton agreed that this was a worthy cause; she expressed concern about not having the scope of the expense for inclusion of low-income children and youth across all schools located in the city of Boulder and living in enclaves. She would like to see more concrete numbers that include all those involved rather than the one group mentioned by Council Member Becker.

Council Member Becker suggested they assist the "Sports for All" group while the broader discussion occurs.

Council Member Jones suggested discussing the issue with employees at Boulder County and considering a partnership.

Council Member Morzel agreed with Council Member Ageton's concerns and added that she was concerned about students that chose open-enrollment from other communities. She asked to see more concrete numbers before taking action on the request. She inquired about why there were not price differentials for residential v. non-residential fees at the recreation centers at the base level, not including annual passes and punch cards.

City Manager Brautigam explained that in general, the pricing was to encourage those using the recreation centers to purchase annual passes rather than paying the drop-in rate.

Council Member Morzel clarified that people who work in Boulder, but do not live in Boulder, can get the recreation passes at the same rate as a resident.

City Manager Brautigam confirmed that was accurate.

Council Member Becker suggested trying the program for one year for the group "Sports for All" while further information was collected.
