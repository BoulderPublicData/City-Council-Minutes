### **5. PUBLIC HEARINGS - 7:50 PM**

### **A. CONSIDERATION OF RESOLUTION NO. 1122 APPROVING THE 2012 CITY OF BOULDER MULTI-HAZARD MITIGATION PLAN UPDATE – 7:50 PM**

Engineering Project Manager Kurt Bauer presented on this item.

Council Member Jones asked if there was likelihood of water restrictions in 2013.

Staff responded that the decision would be made in May.

Mayor Appelbaum asked if the updates were all standard or if there were significant changes that came out of the update.

The major changes to the update included items not completed moving up in priority in relation to those that were removed from the plan as being complete.

Council Member Karakehian recalled a conversation with a citizen regarding the location of their home being only partially inside the city limits and the complications of fire protection. He noted that he would like to bring this topic up to the charter committee.

Mayor Appelbaum asked if there was a long term solution being pursued to protect the western edge of the city where land is primarily privately owned.

Larry Donner, Fire Chief, responded that owners of private property take the precautions that are worthwhile to them to protect their homes.

Mayor Appelbaum clarified that he was interested in getting resources into these areas for fire protection.

The public hearing was opened at 8:14 PM.

1. Ed Byrne – Spoke as a resident impacted by the floodplain and noted that he asked if there are valves on the natural gas lines that would shut off gas in the floodplain if there were a fire.

There being no further speakers, the public hearing was closed at 8:15 PM.

Chief Donner noted that there are many accessible lines, and when there are not, it is generally due to the topography.

Council Member Wilson moved, seconded by Morzel, to adopt Resolution 1122 approving the 2012 City of Boulder Multi-Hazard Mitigation Plan Update. The motion carried 9:0. Vote taken at 8:20 PM.

### **B. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION APPROVING THE MANAGEMENT OF THUNDERBIRD LAKE WATER LEVELS – 8:21 PM**

{4}------------------------------------------------

Director of Parks and Recreation Kirk Kincannon and Planning Superintendent Jeff Dillon presented on this item.

Council Member Cowles asked if previous efforts to maintain the water levels were worthwhile.

Mr. Kincannon noted that there was no data available at that time. There were other problems being addressed through the process as well that were very important to take care of.

Council Member Plass asked what would happen if the data were wrong.

Mr. Kincannon noted that the decision did not prevent the city from adding water if necessary in the future. He commented that if the water levels went down dramatically, it would most likely be in response to a drought.

Council Member Jones asked for clarification as to how much water would have to be added to trigger Council action.

Mr. Kincannon commented that normally those requests go through the Water Department and were decisions staff would make. He noted that staff would continue monitoring the lake levels and update Council on the addition of any large amount of water.

Council Member Ageton asked that staff keep in mind that water is an amenity and she expressed hope that there would be balance between adding additional water and the environmental values of the city. She explained that decisions at Thunderbird Lake could set a precedent in the community.

The public hearing was opened at 8:38 PM.

There being no speakers the public hearing was closed at 8:38 PM.

Council Member Wilson moved, seconded by Mayor Appelbaum, to support a recommendation to maintain Thunderbird Lake at natural surface water level, without the addition of regular potable water and to utilize Adaptive Management Practices to control water quality, visual quality and aquatic species, to preserve the open water levels generally consistent with the 2012 water quality and water levels. The motion carried 9:0. Vote taken at 8:52 p.m.

### **C. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ADOPT ORDINANCE NO. 7895 AMENDING TITLE 9, “LAND USE REGULATION,” B.R.C. 1981, TO CLARIFY THE INCLUSIONARY HOUSING PROGRAM BY ADDING REQUIREMENTS RELATED TO OFF-SITE UNITS, TIMING OF CONSTRUCTION, FLOOR AREA CALCULATION OF ATTACHED AND DETACHED UNITS AND LOCATION OF AFFORDABLE UNITS – 8:53 PM**

Community Development Program Manager and Acting Housing Division Manager Jeff Yegian presented on this item.

Mayor Appelbaum requested staff put the administrative regulations on the website in

{5}------------------------------------------------

a place that was easier to find and search for.

Council Member Becker asked if there was a functional equivalency requirement in affordable housing that would ask developers to utilize, for example, granite countertops in one location and therefore have to use them in all locations.

Mr. Yegian responded that a functional equivalent did not necessarily mean that the exact same item would be used, but there was a requirement in place.

Council Member Becker stated that she did not agree with such a requirement for affordable housing. She noted that affordable housing should be just that, affordable.

Mr. Yegian responded that the requirement for developers requires them to build twenty-percent of any project as affordable or alternative means of compliance that the City Manager can approve. By moving away from the requirement of “mirroring,” the need for exemptions through other means should go down. He further explained that developers can make other arrangements such as building accessible units for the disabled.

Council Member Becker questioned why Council would make the changes being requested knowing that they would be making more changes in the future rather than handling them all at once.

Mayor Appelbaum asked if access to transit and amenities such as grocery stores was going to be considered when evaluating options for offsite units.

Mr. Yegian responded that there were approval requirements that considered such things.

The public hearing was opened at 9:28 PM.

1. Ed Byrne – Speaking on behalf of a client, in opposition to the proposed changes, stating that they would not resolve the problems around affordable housing.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed at 9:32 PM.

Council Member Karakehian praised staff for their efforts in cleaning up the code in regard to offsite units and offering a new approach to working with developers on affordable housing.

Council Member Becker agreed that the changes to offsite units was a good first step.

Council Member Plass moved, seconded by Council Member Jones to amend and adopt Ordinance No. 7895, as reflected in Attachment B, amending Title 9, “Land Use Regulation,” B.R.C. 1981, to clarify the inclusionary Housing program by adding requirements related to off-site units, timing of construction, floor area calculation of attached and detached units, and location of affordable units. The motion carried: 9:0.

### **A. UPDATE FROM THE CITY MANAGER REGARDING THE CREATION OF A WORKING GROUP TO STUDY POTENTIAL ALTERNATIVES TO MUNICIPALIZATION – 10:00 PM**

City Manager Brautigam presented on this item.

Council Member Jones asked what the mission of the group was.

City Manager Brautigam responded that the group would be guided by an outside facilitator paid for by both the city and Xcel. The group was scheduled to meet a total of six times. Its goal was to explore the energy goals put forth in the white paper written by the city in December and explore ideas Xcel had in relation to the municipalization effort. She noted that the goal was not to offer a recommendation on municipalization. The City Council would be able to expect a report from the working group in July.

City Attorney Carr discussed the group’s meetings as falling under the umbrella of “settlement negotiations,” and therefore would be confidential and not open to the public. He also explained that taking that approach protected the city from certain types of litigation.

### **B. CITY COUNCIL EMPLOYEE EVALUATION PROCESS CHECK-IN - 10:23 PM**

Council Members Plass and Morzel presented on this item.

Council Member Cowles noted that he did not want to make the process more administrative than it was. He asked what would be different in relation to goal setting compared to the process that was already in place.

Council Member Plass clarified that the subcommittee wanted to move away from utilizing Council goals as the Council Employee goals. He commented that there are unforeseen circumstances that may prevent certain goals from being achieved and he did not want to penalize the employee for events outside of their control.

Council Member Wilson stated that he would prefer not to expend additional funds to change the process.

Council Member Plass acknowledged that the timeframe being presented was very aggressive and agreed that many of the changes could be implemented the following year.

Council Member Ageton agreed with the idea of reinstating a midyear check in with Council's direct reports. She recalled that it was not a difficult meeting to arrange. She also discussed the benefits of utilizing a consultant for confidentiality purposes. She agreed that the timeline was very ambitious and supported implementing the changes in 2014.

Council Member Becker noted that she would support using a consultant, but felt that the tools used for reviewing employee performance were sufficient.

Council Member Jones agreed.

Mayor Appelbaum stated he also supported use of a consultant, he also commented that he would like to see changes to the tools, specifically how peer groups were selected. He also applauded the process used internally in 2012, stating it was possibly the best and most transparent process he had been a part of in reviewing Council employees.

Council Member Karakehian explained that he was not willing to increase expenditures to make changes to the evaluation process.

Mayor Appelbaum asked for a show of hands to show support for use of a consultant.

{8}------------------------------------------------

The majority agreed.

Council Member Plass asked for direction as to whether Council comments on evaluations would continue to be confidential and not attributed to specific people.
