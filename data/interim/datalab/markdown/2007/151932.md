# **CITY OF BOULDER COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS January 16, 2007**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Acting Mayor Ageton called the regular January 16, 2007 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Councilmembers Bohannan, Gray, McGrath, Polk, Schultheiss and Stoakes.

Those absent were: Mayor Ruzzin and Councilmember Eldridge.

#### **C. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION TO ACCEPT THE AIRPORT MASTER PLAN UPDATE AND AIRPORT MASTER PLAN SUMMARY FOR INCLUSION IN THE BOULDER VALLEY COMPREHENSIVE PLAN. – 7:20 p.m.**

A presentation was provided by Tim Head, Airport Manager, and Noreen Walsh, Senior Transportation Planner.

Mr. Head provided the historical background of the Boulder Municipal Airport, which has been in existence since 1928. He then spoke to the types of aircraft and services provided by the airport.

Ms. Walsh reviewed the Master Plan Update process, noting that the FAA recommends updating every ten years.

Proposed projects and improvement were then reviewed followed by the next steps which include acceptance and implementation of the Boulder Municipal Airport – Master Plan Update.

Planning Board members Simon Mole and John Spitzer spoke to the split vote from the Planning Board, with the dissenting vote asking that Council consider long-term land use issues. The supportive vote indicated that the number of required updates to the Airport Master Plan more than justified approving and maintaining the Boulder Municipal Airport.

{4}------------------------------------------------

The public hearing was opened:

1. Jim Adams addressed two points: 1. weather analysis is faulty, glider and main wing warp are too close together, and Chinook winds due to close proximity to the mountains are not addressed; 2. increased airport activity is creating impacts on community and inconsiderate pilots far surpass the number of fly friendly pilots.
2. Kathy Kelly representing the Master Plan Working Group, noted that the volunteer noise abatement program is a joke, COB has no control over the flight of planes over Boulder, and it is all under the control of the FAA. If the airport expands, the problems will expand. She asked that Council reject the plan.
3. Dan Powers, representing the Boulder Chamber of Commerce, and a participant in the Master Plan Update, voiced his approval of the plan.
4. Kate Fay, representing the Orange Orchard Homeowners Association, noted that noise concerns do exist, and that the city can not control air traffic over Boulder. If additional infrastructure is built, additional air traffic will follow. She suggested phased implementation to ensure that standards are being met.
5. AJ Grant noted that flying trends are changing quickly and the potential impacts on Boulder residents are brightening. Is this airport the best use of this land, given that the city has no control over air traffic? Approving the Master Plan Update gives up any control to the FAA.
6. Tess Adams, a Pinebrook Hills resident, read a statement from Gail Loveman, another Pinebrook Hills resident, stating her displeasure of glider tow planes and the increase in air traffic over the past few years. She asked that the Boulder Municipal Airport be closed.
7. Lawrence Stalla, a pilot, engineer, and Gunbarrel resident, noted that the Boulder Airport is vital to his business, and urged approval of the Master Plan.
8. Jason D. Remuller, a pilot, a Boulder resident, and an atmospheric scientist for the University of Colorado, spoke to the benefit of atmospheric testing available in Boulder. He encouraged Boulder to support Boulder's atmospheric science.
9. Mike Hayard, a pilot who lives right next to the airport and is also very noise sensitive, noted that he believes that he lives in the quietest neighborhood in town. He asked Council to invest in maintaining the Boulder Municipal Airport.

{5}------------------------------------------------

10. Robert Gavlas, owner of Aviation Services, spoke to security issues and the need for more hangers to accommodate quality aircraft. He noted that pilots are trying to be pro-active and work with the airport manager with relation to noise abatement.
11. Alexandra Armitage, part owner of a small plane, spoke to the wonderful experiences offered to people who get the opportunity to fly, and asked Council to please keep the airport open.
12. Steve Richards, who worked for years at the Boulder Airport handling calls, indicated that complaints come from a fairly small minority. He also addressed the change of value and need to protect airplanes that now have a value of over \$100,000.
13. Bruce Hodgkins, noted that buses on Broadway create a lot of noise, trains cause a lot of noise, and that Boulder is an urban area – that means noise.
14. David Campbell, owner of Mile High Gliding, noted that when he bought the company, he increased noise complaints ten-fold because they bought stronger planes for safety concerns. He also noted that they went to great expense to install different propellers that have cut the noise by two-thirds.
15. Jim Thomas, a pilot who was here years ago and then left, noted that when he returned to the area, he was stunned at the poor condition of the Boulder Airport. He noted his amazement that there is so little support for the venue, and spoke to the importance of the airport to his business.
16. Keith Berger, a person who is privileged to fly out of the Boulder Airport noted, “great cities have great airports, it’s part of keeping Boulder a first rate city.”

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember McGrath moved, seconded by Polk, to accept the Airport Master Plan Update document and the one page summary for inclusion into the BVCP. The motion carried 7:0, Eldridge and Ruzzin absent. – 7:20 p.m.
