# **CITY OF BOULDER COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS April 9, 2007**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Ruzzin called the regular April 9, 2007 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Ruzzin, Councilmembers Ageton, Bohannan, Gray, McGrath, Polk, Schultheiss and Stoakes.

Those absent were: Councilmember Eldridge

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE**

1. Amy Tisdale, representing Red Wagon Organic Farm, asked Council to reconsider its position on the South Platte Wells. She noted that asking farmers to pay back 50 years of water is unreasonable and many farmers are going out of business.
2. Cindy Torres, Manager of the Longmont Farmers Market, also asked the city to reconsider the shutdown of wells which effects local produce, which supports local sustainability.
3. Mary Louise Chavers spoke to two public safety issues: she reminded Council that a state license is required of anyone who applies pesticides; and noted that a left hand turn arrow is needed at 14<sup>th</sup> and Canyon.
4. Tim Thomas spoke to the city's January expenditure report and noted that the charge of \$1,123 at Juanita's for a retirement party was not right. Additionally, he noted that over \$10,000 was spent for food in general city wide in that month.
5. James Newcomb spoke to parks in Boulder. He expressed disapproval with the corrugated and multi-tiered fences that are becoming the standard landscaping rather than traditional landscaping of trees, dense vegetation and flowers. He suggested it was time to take back the parks and bring the Olmstead vision back to Boulder.
6. Larry MacDonell, representing Friends of Boulder Open Space, spoke regarding stream flow issue in South Boulder Creek as it relates to working with the Town of Erie. This issue was raised at the last meeting. He asked that Mayor Ruzzin contact the Mayor of Eire directly to express the city's desire to find a collaborative solution to the stream flow issue.
7. Larry Quilling, representing Flycasters and Colorado Trout Unlimited, supported the

{1}------------------------------------------------

comments made by Mr. MacDonell, and stressed the need to preserve adequate streamflow during the winter, and the need to find a way to exchange water with other regional municipalities to maintain that flow.

8. Guy Errickson expressed his dissatisfaction of the investigation regarding conflicts of interest and public access TV.
9. Betsey Martens, representing Boulder Housing Partners (BHP), spoke to the concerns expressed by residents of Glen Willow. She noted that the opportunity from HUD would allow for longer sustainability of valuable housing. She further noted that no decision has been made, and the process is in the beginning phase. BHP is making an effort to meet one-on-one with the residents. At this time, only three residents have been vocal about their concerns.
10. Kurt Nordback spoke regarding Agenda Item 3-C, which conveys right of way to the Colorado Department of Transportation for highway improvements at Highway 52 and 119. He asked Council for an environmental impact statement.

### **City Manager Response:**

City Manager Bruno spoke to the following:

1. He thanked Mr. Thomas for his comments, indicating that the expenditures were being reviewed.
2. Regarding the parks issue, he noted that the proliferation of prairie dogs over the past ten years has required unattractive measures for management purposes.
3. Regarding the Erie pipeline, he indicated that after many discussions with the administration, no headway had been made. However, he was willing to assist in any way to re-engage in discussions with Erie.

### **City Attorney Response:**

City Attorney Calonne clarified that Mr. Errickson had been sent a copy of the conflict of interest decision which determined that Councilmember Polk did not have a conflict of interest with Tony Perri.

### **Council Response:**

Councilmember McGrath indicated support for the Mayor contacting the Mayor of Erie.

### **B. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF EMERGENCY ORDINANCE NO. 7511 TO AUTHORIZE THE CITY MANAGER TO ESTABLISH A NUISANCE MOSQUITO PILOT PROJECT FOR 2007 AND 2008 IN AN AREA EAST OF 55<sup>TH</sup> STREET AD NORTH OF SOUTH BOULDER ROAD AND EXEMPTING SPECIFIC WETLAND AREAS WITHIN THE PILOT AREA FROM THE WETLAND PERMIT REQUIREMENTS OF SECTION 9-3-9, “WETLANDS PROTECTION,” B.R.C. 1981.**

A presentation was provided by: Jonathan Koehn, Manager, Office of Environmental Affairs; Jan Geden, Director, Parks and Recreation; and Mike Patton, Director, Open Space and Mountain Parks.

The public hearing was opened:

1. Mary Louise Chavers, representing the Sierra Club, thanked the county for its willingness to work with the city on this project and reiterated that an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.
2. Debbie Hopkins noted that mosquitoes are so heavy in her neighborhood they have caused people to remain indoors during the summer. She asked that Greenbelt Meadows be added to the pilot program.
3. Kirk Cunningham, representing the Sierra Club and Citizens for Pesticide Reform, read a letter from Scott Spiegal praising city staff for developing the program.
4. Anna Cuppola, representing Resident in the Reserve, Expressed deep concern about mosquitoes and the tremendous increase last summer. She expressed her appreciation that the city is addressing this problem.
5. Jen Sutton voiced support for the pilot program and noted that she feels this approach is a benefit for the entire community.

{4}------------------------------------------------

6. Kirsten Burris expressed concern about the monitoring of BTI but supported the project even with those reservations.
7. Amy Gahran asked how an area that isn't identified as part of the pilot program goes about getting into the program.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Gray moved, seconded by Stoakes, to adopt Emergency Ord. No.7511 to authorize the City Manager to establish a nuisance mosquito pilot project for 2007 and 2008 in an area east of 55<sup>th</sup> Street and north of South Boulder road and exempting specific wetland areas within the pilot area from the wetland permit requirements of Section 9-3-9, "Wetlands Protection," BRC 1981. The motion carried 7:0, Eldridge and Polk absent.

### **A. Report on status of Councilmember Polk from Special Counsel – 8:44 p.m.**

Councilmember Polk recused from this discussion due to a conflict of interest.

Report from Justice Dubofsky and Longmont City Attorney Clayton Douglas.

Council concurred to take no action with regard to calling a special election. Councilmember Polk returned to the dais at this time.

**Mayor Ruzzin then read the following statement into the record to provide the community with context as to why this issue was coming forward at this time:**

“At its business meeting of October 3, 2006, the Council provided a detailed statement to the community about the role that it would be playing as it related to Councilmember Polk’s tenure on the Boulder City Council. We outlined two steps to the process: 1) We first needed to await the resolution of Councilmember Polk’s case by the District Attorney. That outcome was realized on February 9, 2007 when Mr. Polk pled guilty to reckless driving; 2) The second step involved applying that resolution to the City Charter. This step has just been completed with the acceptance, this evening by the City Council, of the analysis provided to us by our special counsel.

I would like to remind the community that, as a body, we have had a clear understanding of the Charter requirements that dictated the process relating to Councilmember Polk’s service on the Council. We also were, and are now, in clear understanding of the role we as public officials and leaders play in the community. I would like to re-read a part of the statement that Council made on this subject at that same October 3<sup>rd</sup> meeting.

The arrest of Councilmember Polk is a serious matter. As he himself stated this evening, as elected members of the city council we have an obligation to act responsibly at all times. As community representatives we are indeed held to a higher standard. And I want to assure the

{6}------------------------------------------------

public that no one on this council takes that charge lightly. We do have a responsibility as public officials to not only conduct ourselves ethically and honorably, but to also remember that our actions and behaviors send a message to the young people and residents of this community about right and wrong.

It has been over six months since that meeting, and with the City Council's role in this process drawing to a close I felt it important that we all be reminded of the steps that have been taken over that time period.”

#### **Councilmember Polk then read his statement for the record:**

“I apologize again for the conduct that led to my arrest on September 25<sup>th</sup>. It has caused the City Council, the Boulder community, my family, and me considerable embarrassment and expense.

I join my colleagues in their condemnation of driving under the influence of drugs or alcohol. However, I was not under the influence of drugs or alcohol at the time of my arrest, nor was I driving in a manner that put others at risk. The entirety of the evidence in this case, which goes well beyond what has been publicly reported and commented on by the media, supports these assertions. Regardless, I accept full responsibility for this matter, and I regret the distractions from business of the council that it has produced.

At the City Council meeting following my arrest, I promised to immediately seek appropriate professional therapy. I also voluntarily submitted to drug testing. Despite the family tragedy and personal health challenges that followed, I kept those promises, while continuing to meet the responsibilities and duties of my office, my family, and my business.

Moving forward, it is my intention to fully comply with the sentence imposed by the court, and to behave with the dignity appropriate to my office, while continuing to serve the citizens who elected me. It is also my hope that others, particularly the young members of our community, will learn from my experience and exercise sound judgment and respect for the law.”

Councilmember Ageton moved, seconded by McGrath to send a letter of reprimand to Councilmember Polk as amended after Council discussion to read:

April 9, 2007

Letter of Reprimand to Council Member Polk

Dear Councilmember Polk:

After due consideration of the facts of your case and the analysis of Special Counsel, the Council has accepted that your recent conviction for reckless driving is not the type of crime that the voters who enacted Article II, Section 8 of the City Charter intended to create a vacancy on the Council.

Your conduct was, however, a violation of state law and community values, and an act that showed disregard for the public's safety. While you were convicted for reckless

{7}------------------------------------------------

driving, the arrest report and original charge have created a community perception that you were operating a motor vehicle while affected by drugs. The Council wants to be absolutely clear with you and with the community we represent, that we condemn in the strongest terms the operation of a motor vehicle while under the influence of drugs or alcohol.

As elected officials and community leaders, we have an obligation to exemplify and uphold the legal and ethical expectations established by the law. When we disregard these, we undermine both respect for the law and our credibility as public officials.

Our letter to you is intended to express our deep disapproval of your conduct, as well as to reaffirm our commitment to the community's laws, values and safety.

Sincerely,  
Mark Ruzzin  
Mayor

### **D. Report on the US 36 Mayors and Commissioners Coalition trip to Washington D.C. – Councilmember Ageton – 10:25 p.m.**

{9}------------------------------------------------

**D.C. – Councilmember Ageton – 10:25 p.m.**

This trip focused on joining efforts for regional funding. The full coalition was present and it began by updating congressional members on the progress to date on US 36: Draft Environmental Impact Statement, the separation of rail from the BRT and the Northwest Rail Corridor Study. The group met with both Representatives Udall and Pearlmutter and received very strong support. They also pursued a funding platform to advocate with CDOT to address the multimodel improvement which would need to be addressed on Table Mesa once Rapid Transit comes on line. This is expected to be approximately a 3.5 million dollar project. The group was also looking for a separate pot of money for bus transit rather than all the funding focused on highway improvements.
