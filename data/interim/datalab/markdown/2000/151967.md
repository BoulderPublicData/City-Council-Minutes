# **CITY OF BOULDER CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS March 7, 2000**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Toor called the regular March 7, 2000 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Toor and Councilmembers Corson, Eldridge, Havlick, Lopez, Mock, and Poinsatte. Councilmembers Morzel and Riggle were absent.

## **2. CITIZEN PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE - 6:05 p.m.**

1. John Seaborn, 5560 Boulder Hills Drive - re: As president of the Boulder Soaring Society, urged Council to remove the Boulder Airport site from the list of city properties identified for review for possible alternative uses.
2. Lisa Mainero, 1326 N. Cedar Brook Road - re: support for the Boulder Airport - listed several community benefits of the local airport.
3. David Silberman, 2727 Folsom Avenue - re: North Boulder Power line, noted that there seemed to be large discrepancies in cost estimates for the undergrounding project.
4. Ben Lattora, 7488 Old Mill Road - re: cost of golf fee at Flatirons course for out of city senior resident. Noted that it is more expensive than four other courses in the Boulder area.
5. Mary Boisseau, 6292 Arapahoe - re: negative impacts of airport noise on local residents.
6. Robert Sharpe, 5995 Marshall Drive - re: numerous local school issues. Also asked if there is consideration for moving the site for the Palo Park school further out. If so, he would be opposed.
7. David Wagner, 5280 Holmes Place - re: Traffic Mitigation, appalled by the lack of progress to date towards this critical goal.
8. Frank Ohrtman, 2600 9<sup>th</sup> Street #5A - re: C.A.T.V. temporary contract. Encouraged allowing the contract to lapse thus allowing CATV to become a private enterprise.
9. Ricky Weiser, 4020 N. 75<sup>th</sup> Street - re: 1) supported item 3-d, 2) item 6-a, *concerned about view*, 3) opposed to traffic mitigation devices, and 4) still concerned about 18 wheelers on Pearl Parkway.
10. Lisah Brown, 1406 Pine Street #3 - re: traffic circles on 14<sup>th</sup> and Pine. As resident, opposed to being denied vote on these structures.

11. Warren Hultquist, 1820 Mapleton -re: NTMP memo to Council on March 2<sup>nd</sup>, noted that process was unfair in that residents on Pine and Balsam were not allowed to vote on devices.
12. Evan Ravitz, University Hill - re: undo influence from Transportation Division on Boulder residents.
13. Rob Smoke, 2445 19<sup>th</sup> Street #13 - re: J&B billboard truck atrocity, noting that young people are being targeted by this subversive advertising.
14. Shaun Dalrymple, 3745 Silver Plume Lane - Chair of C.A.T.V. Provided an update regarding the current operations of Channel 54.
15. Richard Hayward, 1750 Lombardy Drive - re: stated opposition to the traffic circles on 14<sup>th</sup> and Pine.
16. Richard Peddie, 4750 Table Mesa Drive - re: current director and soon to be chair of C.A.T.V. informed Council that in his opinion the current Board of Directors is more able than previous boards to succeed in providing quality service.

### **Staff Response:**

City Manager Ron Secrist responded as follows:

- 1) Regarding pending review of City properties, the Study Session on March 14<sup>th</sup> and then a public hearing on March 21<sup>st</sup> will be focused on the 5 year Comprehensive plan update. Part of those discussions will include alternative use of city owned properties with the exception of Open Space and Mountain Parks.
- 2) Staff will be asked to contact Mr. Silverman regarding clarification of the undergrounding costs associated with the North Boulder Power line.
- 3) Staff will look into the non-resident golf fees and policies.
- 4) Regarding the Palo Park school site, Deputy Mayor Mock clarified that the School Board is discussing other locations that may be more appropriate for a new elementary school but no decisions have been made.
- 5) Regarding the traffic circles on Pine and Balsam, the current action was taken as result of specific direction from the City Council in late 1999. He then reviewed the public process that will take place prior to final decision from Council.
- 6) The C.A.T.V. performance review is underway. Should the review not be completed by March 31, the contract will be extended to allow for its completion.

City Attorney Joe de Raismes responded to the concerns about the J&B Billboard truck by concurring that it appears to be a violation of the city sign code and that his office will pursue the matter.

## 6. **MATTERS FROM MAYOR AND MEMBERS OF COUNCIL:** - 8:15 p.m.

### **A. POTENTIAL CALL-UPS:**

1. **SITE REVIEW AND USE REVIEW FOR A 200 ROOM, 55 FOOT TALL HOTEL, A 55 FOOT TALL CIVIC USE BUILDING, AND A 656 SPACE UNDERGROUND PARKING GARAGE AT 9TH STREET AND CANYON BOULEVARD. LAST OPPORTUNITY FOR CALL-UP: 3/7/00. WIP DATE:2/23/00 . APPROVED BY THE PLANNING BOARD (6-1; T. KRUEGER OPPOSED.)**

Councilmember Havlick moved, seconded by Poinsatte, to call-up the Site Review and Use Review for a 200 room, 55 foot tall hotel, a 55 foot tall civic use building, and a 656 space underground parking garage at 9th Street and Canyon Boulevard.

2. **DOWNTOWN MALL MASTER PLAN CONCEPTUAL DESIGN. LAST OPPORTUNITY FOR CALL-UP” 3-7-00. WIP DATE: 2-10-00. UNANIMOUS APPROVAL BY DMC.**

### **B. LEGISLATIVE ISSUES** - 8:20 p.m.

City Attorney Joe de Raismes provided an overview of pending bills before the State Legislature concentrating specifically on H.B. 1223, the Growth Management Bill sponsored by CML and CCI, noting that although the city has traditionally supported the intent of the bill there are numerous concerns regarding specific language in the amendments by Representative Tom Plant.

Mayor Toor moved, seconded by Councilmember Havlick to support H.B. 1223, with the amendments proposed by Representative Plant while acknowledging the concerns outlined by the City Attorney and directing him to work toward further amendments which would clarify the language of the bill.

## 7. **PUBLIC COMMENT:** - 8:50 p.m.

## 8. **FINAL DECISIONS:** - 8:55 p.m.
