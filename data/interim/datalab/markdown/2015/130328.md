### **C. CONTINUATION OF THE SECOND READING AND MOTION TO ADOPT ORDINANCE NO. 8034 AMENDING THE CAPITAL FACILITIES IMPACT FEE IN SECTION 4-20-62, “AND CHAPTER 8–9, B.R.C. 1981 BY THE ADDITION OF A NEW AFFORDABLE HOUSING LINKAGE FEE ON NON-RESIDENTIAL DEVELOPMENT, AND SETTING FORTH RELATED DETAILS; 8:21 PM.**

The presentation of this item was provided by Deputy Director of Community Sustainability and Planning, Susan Richstone.

The public hearing on this item was opened at 8:52 PM.

1. Andrew Bush, expressed that he thought Council had the “cart was before the horse” or the ordinance before the public participation on this issue. He explained that the property owners were not present because of the lack of formal public process as well as other key stakeholders. He encouraged doing a new and current study for the linkage fee and cities implement these fees collect over the average life of the lease and based on submittals and not approvals.
2. Dick Harris, represented PLAN Boulder County and enthusiastically supported the ordinance for affordable housing,
3. Bill Holicky, served on the Affordable Housing Task Force, explained that it would be really hard to reach the affordable housing goal until the affordable housing tax was supported on a broad based stand point. This was a community issue not a growth issue. The other issue was how to assess the linkage fee to developers and small business owners.
4. Angelique Espinoza, Public Affairs Director for the Boulder Chamber of Commerce, and also served on the Affordable Housing Task Force explained the gap between affordable housing, availability and how the market was very complicated problem. She requested consideration not only on the impacts of development to the community but also the contributions that development brings. She suggested delaying the fees, completing the study and creating possible exemptions for the fees in certain cases.
5. Adrian Sopher, spoke for his client, Scott Holton Development Properties, and was about ready to resubmit the site review for his project. He was concerned

{5}------------------------------------------------

about the housing and office space implementation and cost of the linkage fees. He requested clarification on the fees prior to adoption as his project will take a big hit.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed at 9:10 PM.

Council Member Plass moved, seconded by Council Member Weaver to adopt Ordinance No. 8034 amending the capital facilities impact fee in Section 4-20-62, “and Chapter 8–9, B.R.C. 1981 by the addition of a new affordable housing linkage fee on non-residential development, and setting forth related details.

Questions of Council Members:

**Asst City Attorney, David Gehr** explained the tech doc trigger associated with this project, Pg 122, had a phase in for building permits, adding the tech doc provision.

**Council Member Morzel** asked about the phase-in application fee. Susan Richstone answered that the impact fee should be collected as close to actual impact so that the money mitigates the impact. Council Member Morzel supported the motion and agreed that the head tax is a separate item.

**Council Member Jones** needed additional clarity on what the impact fees would be if the project had some affordability measures with it.

**Assistant City Attorney, David Gehr** responded that the approach possibly might be to have an affordable housing credit to comply.

**Council Member Cowles** thought it should be phased in. He was concerned about the impact on small business developers that wanted to expand. Speakers Sopher, Holicky and Bush were saying let’s approach this in a different way. He thought a head tax was the way to go or wait until the study was done.

**Council Member Karakehian** considered this a mistake and rushing would be wrong. This was not the “Boulder Way.” He wanted a middle-of-the-road-option that would give the projects already started a fair chance.

**Council Member Young** thought that there are impacts regardless. She commented about the head tax and explained that the amount would depend on the rate on an annual basis.

**Council Member Shoemaker** was concerned that this would increase the value of properties. He agreed that it needs to wait and possibly go with a head tax. The affordable housing needed to be addressed through head tax or development fees.

**Mayor Appelbaum** remarked that the impact of development fees were not a way

{6}------------------------------------------------

to calculate growth. This limited sphere could do a reasonably good job of measuring the impact by capturing some portion of growth related to capital components, but the current fees were based on an older study. Should we wait for the next update? He stated that there is never a good time to start the trigger point. The head tax and the impact fees were very different items. Head tax was employment related and had different purposes than impact fees.

**Council Member Plass** wanted to find the “sweet spot” for everyone. He suggested drawing up a reasonable balance and bringing fairness to people who have projects already in the pipeline. The Head tax item was a separate topic.

**Council Member Weaver** offered a friendly amendment, accepted by the mover of the motion, to include Amendment 3 (h) Housing Excise Tax as presented on Packet Page 123.

**Assistant City Attorney, David Gehr** would prepare the ordinance with the amendments.

Vote was taken on the motion to adopt Ordinance No. 8034 amending the capital facilities impact fee in Section 4-20-62, “and Chapter 8–9, B.R.C. 1981 by the addition of a new affordable housing linkage fee on non-residential development, as amended with the Housing Excise Tax Credit and setting forth related details. The motion carried 9:0 at 10:20 PM.

Staff continued the presentation for the preparation of new studies and move forward with an RFP to do the work.

**City Manager, Jane Brautigam** reminded Council that at the Study Session the direction was come back before Council recess with figures from the head tax as a revenue generating mechanism. Staff was not doing any calculations that would come from the work of a consultant.

**Mayor Appelbaum** asked what types of things could the head tax fund? He wanted some information before the break. He was interested in the study of the consultants and best practices and would like to use the information to assist in the comprehensive look at fees.

**Council Member Weaver** suggested making a list of the types of things the city needs and then seeing if the revenue would support it.

**Council Member Cowles** advised the RFP to include the approach of looking for new ideas.

**Council Member Young** wanted the RFP to include input of cutting edge methodologies.

{7}------------------------------------------------

Council asked to look at the RFP when it was ready. The RFP (Request for Proposal) would be sent to Council via a web link or a copy for viewing. Deputy Director of Community Planning and Sustainability Susan Richstone asked for additional comments or suggestions from Council via email.

**Chris Meschuck** responded Council regarding the bid process. He used a vendor named Rocky Mountain Bid Net which had a cost for membership. Therefore, he would provide a duplicate copy on the website for Council and for public accessibility.
