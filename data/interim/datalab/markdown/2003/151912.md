# **CITY OF BOULDER** **COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS** **March 4, 2003** **6:00 p.m.**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Toor called the regular March 4, 2003 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Toor and Councilmembers Corson, Eldridge, Havlick, Mock, Poinsatte, Riggle and Ruzzin. Councilmember Morzel was absent.

## **2. CITIZEN PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE**

1. Sierra Cole, Boulder, 80302; re: traffic radar - (Photo Red light/Photo Radar) maintains that this is unconstitutional and that we are sacrificing safety for revenues.
2. Matthew Plummer, 600 Arapahoe Ave. #4, 80302; re: Peak Association of the Arts seeking assistance from the city as it faces Chapter 11 or even Bankruptcy.
3. John M.. Lewis, 1028 Pleasant St. #2, 80302; Also speaking in opposition to Photo radar/Red light program siting that studies in British Columbia indicate that it does not improve safety.
4. Joe Sierra, Boulder; also spoke to Photo Red light and Photo radar indicating that it can be abused as has been the case in San Diego, and also expressed that it is an invasion of privacy.
5. Jim Jenkins, 802 Brooklawn Dr., 80303; re: water rates and the importance of looking carefully at this issue. Expressed concern over the cost of Kentucky Blue Grass as opposed to other alternatives.

### **STAFF RESPONSE:**

City Attorney de Raismes clarified that photo radar and photo red light are constitutional.

City Manager Frank Bruno indicated that he would verify with staff whether yellow light turns have been reduced or whether the city has a speed on Green program.

### **COUNCIL RESPONSE:**

Council asked for information from the January 19<sup>th</sup> Water Rates public meeting to be included in the packet for 2<sup>nd</sup> Reading of the Monthly Water User Charges and Conservation Measures Ordinance (Ordinance #7270) scheduled for the March 18<sup>th</sup> council meeting.

## 4. **MATTERS FROM THE CITY MANAGER:**

### **A. UPDATE REGARDING THE ECONOMIC VITALITY ACTION GROUP. - 6:30PM**

City Manager Bruno informed Council that at the first meeting the rather narrow scope of the six month effort which is to suggest tools, strategies and alternatives that might assist in enhancing business opportunities and sales tax revenues was described to the group. A clear willingness of the group was apparent and the group agreed to meet every 2 weeks with its first task being to list the perceived advantages and disadvantages of doing business in Boulder. Additional input from PLAN Boulder and other entities will also be included. Mr. Bruno indicted that he will continue to provide input to Council on the progress of this group.

### **B. RTD UPDATE. - 6:35PM**

Transportation Director Tracy Winfree provided an update to Council regarding the staff recommendation from RTD the Board of Directors to discontinue the neighborhood and business Eco Pass programs by 2004. She also presented Council with a draft letter to RTD expressing strong opposition to the elimination of these programs.

Mayor Toor moved, seconded by Havlick, to approve the draft letter of opposition to RTD regarding the proposed elimination of the Neighborhood and Business Eco pass programs.

Council asked that staff provide regular updates to Council on this critical issue as additional information comes forward..

{4}------------------------------------------------

Mayor Toor offered a friendly amendment, accepted by the maker and seconder of the motion, to ask Boulder Valley School District to work with the City of Boulder to evaluate opportunities to use Education Excise Tax funds and to house community activities in schools to create a financially feasible way to keep Neighborhood schools open.

Vote was taken on the motion to adopt Resolution No. 924 regarding the City's position on the Boulder Valley School District's proposed school closures and consolidations as amended. The motion carried 8:0; Morzel absent.
