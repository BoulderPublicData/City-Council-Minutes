### A. Update on the **Marshall Fire Transition to Recovery**

{1}------------------------------------------------

City Manager Nuria Rivera-Vandermyde provided an update on the City of Boulder’s efforts to support the neighboring communities affected and what City neighborhoods and open spaces were affected by the fires and strong winds.

Boulder County Office of Disaster Management Mike Chard provided Council with an update on the fires affecting the County, the emergency response overview, community notification and evacuation efforts, sheltering locations, disaster assessment, and the current services provided to those impacted by these fires. He also thanked the City of Boulder for their immediate and continuous support.

Boulder Chamber of Commerce representative John Tayer acknowledged the City of Boulder’s continuous support to the fire’s impact on businesses and outlined the resources made available through the Chamber. He also stressed that price gouging has been an issue with disasters such as these and any citizen who feels they may be experiencing price gouging to report it to the authorities.

Various staff answered questions from Council.

## **7. Matters from the City Attorney**

- A. Request for a **“Nod of Five” for staff to develop a comprehensive ordinance regulating firearms and firearm related issues** broadly relying on model legislation provided by regional partners and national gun restriction advocates. Separate and apart from the request for a Nod of Five, staff will be bringing forward an ordinance on January 18, 2022 to specifically address the city's current assault weapons ordinance to bring it into compliance with the new state statute

City Attorney Teresa Taylor-Tate presented to Council a list of associated subjects to include in the ordinance. Council reviewed each of the topics and selected the areas they felt strongly should be included in the ordinance.

The “Nod of Five” was given by all of Council unanimously.
