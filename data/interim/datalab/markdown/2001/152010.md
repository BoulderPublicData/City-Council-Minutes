# **CITY OF BOULDER CITY COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS**

**FEBRUARY 6, 2001**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL - 6:00 p.m.**

Mayor Toor called the regular February 6, 2001 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Toor and Councilmembers Corson, Eldridge, Havlick, Mock, Morzel, Poinsatte and Riggle. Councilmember Lopez was absent

### **A. SPECIAL RECOGNITION OF MEDAL OF SCIENCE WINNERS: DR. GILBERT WHITE AND DR. SUSAN SOLOMON.**

Mayor Toor presented special declarations for each of the recipients of the Medal of Science and gave them each a gift thanking them for their contributions to our community as well as applauding their national achievements.

### **B. SPECIAL PRESENTATION TO BOULDER CITY ATTORNEY JOSEPH N. DE RAISMES, AS OUTSTANDING CITY ATTORNEY OF THE YEAR.**

Mayor Toor presented Joseph de Raismes with a plaque designating him as the Outstanding City Attorney of the Year by the Metro City Attorney's Association and thanked him for his continued excellent service to the City of Boulder.

## **5. MATTERS FROM THE CITY ATTORNEY - None**

## **6. MATTERS FROM MAYOR AND MEMBERS OF COUNCIL - 7:25 p.m.**

### **A. CONSIDERATION OF A RESOLUTION SUPPORTING THE EXECUTIVE ORDER, DESIGNATING 58 MILLION ACRES OF FOREST SERVICE LAND AS ROADLESS AREAS.**

Councilmember Mock moved, seconded by Toor, to adopt Resolution No. 875 supporting the executive order, designating 58 million acres of forest service land as roadless areas.

Council asked that this be copied to the US Forest Service and the Secretary of Agriculture in addition to President Bush and members of the Colorado Congressional delegation.

### **B. CONSIDERATION OF A RESOLUTION SUPPORTING THE ENVIRONMENTAL PROTECTION AGENCY'S FINAL RULE FOR HEAVY- DUTY DIESEL FUEL AND ENGINE EMISSIONS.**

Councilmember Morzel moved, seconded by Eldridge to adopt Resolution No. 876 supporting the Environmental Protection Agency's final rule for heavy-duty diesel fuel and engine emissions as amended through a friendly amendment to add ,“Whereas, the City of Boulder is located in an airshed that is subject to frequent inversions, resulting in the concentration of air pollutants that adversely affect the health of our citizenry.”

### **C. PLIGHT OF NON-PROFIT AVAILABILITY OF OFFICE SPACE - Councilmember Corson**

After discussion, Council concurred to direct staff to define the scope of the problem of the Public Non-Profits in obtaining affordable office space and to then to identify possible approaches, perhaps through re-development projects.

{3}------------------------------------------------

### **D. MANAGING COMMERCIAL GROWTH PUBLIC PROCESS.**

Planning Director Peter Pollock and Planner Ruth McHeyser led a discussion that was continued from the January 30<sup>th</sup> Council Retreat regarding the issue of public process surrounding Commercial Growth Management. Public input was a primary theme that Council stressed as vital and a longer timeline seemed the most appropriate method to do it correctly. Target date of 2002 for completion was the Council concensus.

## **7. PUBLIC COMMENT:**

## **8. FINAL DECISIONS:**

Vote was taken on the motion to approve Consent Agenda Items 3-A through 3-F with the exception of item 3-C which was removed from the Consent Agenda.. The motion carried: 8 - 0, Lopez absent.

Vote was taken on the motion to adopt Resolution No. 875 supporting the executive order, designating 58 million acres of forest service land as roadless areas. The motion carried: 6 - 2, Lopez absent, Eldridge and Riggle opposed.

Vote was taken on the motion to adopt Resolution No. 876 supporting the Environmental Protection Agency's final rule for heavy-duty diesel fuel and engine emissions as amended through a friendly amendment to add ,“Whereas, the City of Boulder is located in an airshed that is subject to frequent inversions, resulting in the concentration of air pollutants that adversely affect the health of our citizenry.” The motion carried: 8 - 0, Lopez absent.

### Public Hearing:

- 1) Jeep Campbell, 4395 Grinnell - Re: In complete support of the project with increased density. Urged more senior units. Suggested mandating some sort of passive solar in each unit.
- 2) Mark Young, 570 Yuma Circle - Re: also supports project with increased density.

Councilmember Morzel moved, seconded by Poinsatte, to “amend” Ordinance No. 7116 amending the MXR-D (Mixed Density Residential - Developing) to allow a density bonus for affordable housing in the MXR-D zoning district as follows: To maintain the maximum density in the MXR-D zoning district at 10 units per acre, but allow an increase to the maximum density when more affordable housing is provided as follows: up to 15 units per acre when at least 30% of the dwelling units are permanently affordable, and up to 18 units per acre if at least 35% of the units are permanently affordable “and up to 20 units per acre if at least 40% of the units are permanently affordable.” The motion carried: 8 - 0, Lopez absent.

#### **B. ALLOW RESTAURANTS NO LARGER THAN 1,000 SQUARE FEET THROUGH USE REVIEW IN THE MU-D ZONING DISTRICT.**

Councilmember Eldridge moved, seconded by Havlick, to “amend” Ordinance No. 7118 amending the MU-D (Mixed-use Developing) zoning districts to allow restaurants no larger than “1,500 square feet by- right in the MU-D zoning district.” The motion carried: 7-1, Mock opposed, Lopez absent.

These ordinances will require third readings on February 20<sup>th</sup> on the Consent Agenda.
