## 5. **PUBLIC HEARINGS:**

- A. **SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE No. 7496 AMENDING BOULDER REVISED CODE SECTION 5-1-1, “DEFINITIONS,” AND SECTION 5-2-4 “GENERAL PENALTIES,” TO INCREASE THE MAXIMUM FINE FOR BIAS MOTIVATED CRIMES TO \$2,000. – 7:17 p.m.**

City Attorney Calonne introduced the time, noting that his job was to bring back an enforceable ordinance at Council’s direction. Having reviewed Supreme Court caselaw, he indicated he was confident that the ordinance was enforceable.

The public hearing was opened:

1. Judd Golden, representing Boulder ACLU, indicated a number of concerns with the ordinance. He noted the Colorado Constitution would likely be the basis for challenge on the ordinance. He maintained that Colorado State Law already meets the requirements desired by Council. In addition, a state connection provides for potential felony convictions, supervised probation and the ability to claim punitive damages. State law sets a much higher standard to the ordinance which also seems to be the desire of the Council.
2. Ray Stewart, 325 Broadway, representing BCU, the Human Relations Commission (HRC), BIG, Martin Luther King Planning Committee, and a sponsor of the Youth Opportunities Advisory Board (YOAB), quoted “It’s always the right time to do the right thing” – Martin Luther King, Jr. He asked Council to please adopt the ordinance.
3. Audrey Fishman Franklin, 3761 Moffit Ct., representing Boulder Community United, urged Council to approve the ordinance. She noted the uniqueness of the Boulder and the importance of taking this extra step.

{5}------------------------------------------------

4. Janet Heimer, 4265 N. 26<sup>th</sup> St., representing Boulder Community United, expressed the importance of making a statement that bias motivated crimes will not be tolerated.
5. Sara-Jane Cohen, representing the Anti-Defamation League, voiced support for the ordinance with the addition of crimes against persons and property.
6. Bill Cohen, representing Boulder Community United, voiced support for the ordinance and noted that he had distributed a memo addressing the ordinance which touched on the following: 1. Crimes against property should be included; 2. Numerous cities have anti-bias motivated crime ordinances with overlapping state or federal law that have been upheld in court; 3. It is constitutional as long as intentional motivation is proved; and, 4. Fighting words must also carry the burden of intentional motivation.
7. Jennifer Seidman, 2969 Baseline Rd., 2<sup>nd</sup> Floor, representing the Anti-Defamation League, noted that bias motivated crimes are directed not only against the individuals, but also towards the groups they may represent. The message of no tolerance to those who perpetrate crimes of this nature is vital. She urged the adoption of the ordinance.
8. Barry Satlow, 1951 Vista Dr., representing Boulder County ACLU, asked how prosecuting crimes at a lower level court with lower penalties sends a stronger message. He noted support for leaving this important issue in the hands of state law.
9. Betty Ball, representing the Rocky Mountain Peace and Justice Center and a member of Boulder Community United, spoke in support of the ordinance.
10. Robert Sharpe, 5995 Marshall Dr., voiced concern about bias intent being proven. He noted support of the ACLU position and encouraged Council to adopt a resolution supporting state law, and making public outreach to establish the policy that Boulder will not tolerate bias motivated crimes.
11. Tim Thomas, 805 McIntire St., representing CU Environmental Center Environmental Steering Committee, spoke in support of the ordinance noting that the statement is an important step.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Schultheiss moved, seconded by Stoakes, to approve Ordinance No. 7496 amending Boulder Revised Code Section 5-1-1, "Definitions," and Section 5-2-4 "General Penalties," to increase the maximum fine for Bias Motivated Crimes to \$2,000.

Councilmember Bohannan offered friendly amendment to page 5A-2 to add to Section 1-A, the issue of age, disability, gender variance and identity. The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment.

{6}------------------------------------------------

Councilmember Bohannan offered friendly amendment to page #5A-3 to include language under findings, acknowledging the City's anti bias policy. The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment.

Councilmember Bohannan moved, seconded by Gray, to include crimes against property as an amendment on page 5A-4. The motion carried 8:1, McGrath opposed.

Councilmember McGrath offered friendly amendment to page 5A-5 to add Section 1(c) language to the end of 5-2-4 (d) (leaving the language in Section 1 (c) as well). The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment.

Vote was taken on the motion by Councilmember Schultheiss, seconded by Stoakes, to approve Ordinance No. 7496 increasing the maximum fine for bias motivated crimes to \$2,000 as amended. The motion carried 9:0.

### **B. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE NO. 7491 DESIGNATING THE HOUSE AND A PORTION OF THE SITE AT 1802 CANYON BLVD, TO BE KNOWN ALSO AS THE MONTGOMERY-CALABRESE HOUSE, AS AN INDIVIDUAL LANDMARK UNDER THE CITY OF BOULDER HISTORIC PRESERVATION CODE. THE HEARING ON THIS ITEM WILL BE HELD UNDER THE QUASI-JUDICIAL PROCEDURES OF CHAPTER 1-3, BRC 1981. – 8:50 p.m.**

All speakers were sworn in by the City Clerk and a brief presentation of the quasi-judicial proceedings was provided by Chris Meschuk from the Planning Department.

The City Clerk asked for any *exparte* disclosures.

Councilmember Gray noted that Tim Plass from the Landmarks Preservation Advisory **Board** (LPAB) indicated that the vote of LPAB was 3:2 in favor of the staff recommendation.

There were no further disclosures.

A presentation was provided by planning department staff Chris Meschuk and James Hewatt that outlined the historical significance of this property and the history to date of the proposal.

Leonard May, a member of the Landmark Preservation Advisory Board (LPAB), provided a report on the LPAB deliberations and decisions.

Applicant Norman Murphy brought forward speakers in support of his case.

Steve Harres, 2336 Canyon Blvd., spoke to the intent to landmark and move the building. He noted that when the move began, it became apparent that the building was in such poor condition it could not be accomplished.

{7}------------------------------------------------

John Geltner, 2590 Woodstock Place, a structural engineer, stated the house was crumbling and that this would not be a restoration but rather, would be a complete reconstruction of an unsafe house.

Todd Schultz, Benchmark Contractor, spoke to his inability to hire sub-contractors to work on the building given its dangerous condition.

David Tansue, 5525 Racket Lane, the Structural engineer hired by the city provided an overview of his report to council.

The public hearing was opened:

1. Andy Johnson, 920 Lincoln Ave., indicated that in his opinion, the house would need to be completely rebuilt on the interior to bring it up to standards and it would likely be cost prohibitive.
2. Norman Murphy, representing Canyon Properties, spoke to the efforts to meet the landmarking requirement but halted when it became apparent that each step of the move led to more damage.
3. Tim Thomas, 805 McIntire St., asked how Boulder plans to balance Historic preservation and affordable housing.

In response to Council's inquiry, Brent Bean from the Planning Department spoke to the process/ability of the applicant to move forward with the project if the property wasn't landmarked, indicating that the project was approved prior to the landmarking and could move forward based on that approval.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember McGrath moved, seconded by Eldridge, to not adopt Ordinance No. 7491 and deny the landmarking of 1802 Canyon Blvd. The motion carried 7:2, Gray and Stoakes opposed.

Councilmember Ageton moved, seconded by McGrath, to suspend the rules and continue the meeting. The motion carried 7:2, Eldridge and Stoakes opposed. – 10:32 p.m.
