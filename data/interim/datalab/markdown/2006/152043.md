# **CITY OF BOULDER COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS July 11, 2006**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Ruzzin called the regular July 11, 2006 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Mayor Ruzzin and Councilmembers Ageton, Bohannan, Eldridge, Gray, McGrath, Polk, Schultheiss and Stoakes.

### **A. ONE BOOK, ONE BOULDER KICK-OFF**

Mayor Ruzzin indicated that the library is getting ready to launch its second One Book, One Boulder project. The book that has been chosen is When the Emporer Was Divine by Julie Otsuka, and the author will be visiting Boulder October 26-27 for two community presentations (an author dinner at CU and a free presentation/discussion at Fairview HS).

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE – 6.15 p.m.**

1. Kirk Cunningham, 977 7<sup>th</sup> Street, representing the Indian Peaks Chapter of the Sierra Club, indicated endorsement of the Clean Drinking Water initiative. Concerns regarding current water quality were raised.
2. Laurie Thayer, 440 London Ave, Lafayette, asked Council to table any action on the Urban Wildlife Master Plan until after the completion of the Grasslands Management Plan. She also spoke in opposition to the killing of any prairie dogs.
3. Andrew Aitchison, 1111 College Ave., representing University of Colorado Student Union, introduced himself and indicated his desire to work with the City on issues of mutual concern.
4. Pierre Brunschwig. 4150 Darley Ave., a physician from Longmont, voiced his support of the Clean Drinking Water initiative due to the presence of lead and arsenic in Boulder's drinking water.
5. Viola and Iris Landers, 870 35<sup>th</sup> St., also voiced their support of the Clean Drinking Water initiative.
6. Alison Birchell, PO Box 3761, spoke to the Clean Drinking Water initiative, indicating that after 50 years, it's time to have this important community discussion.
7. Deirdre Butler, PO Box 1337, Lyons, urged Council to table the Study Session Summary from the May 23, 2006 session regarding the prairie dog component of the Urban Wildlife Management Plan. She spoke of the need to provide additional acreage to preserve prairie dogs. She also spoke to public sentiment in favor of saving the prairie dogs.

{1}------------------------------------------------

8. Lindsey Sterling Krank, 3250 O'Neal Circle, asked Council to deny acceptance of the May 23<sup>rd</sup> Study Session Summary indicating support of the Grasslands Management Plan completion prior to the Urban Wildlife Plan. She asked for an extension of one month to find relocation sites.
9. Paul Tabolt, University of Colorado, Regent Hall, Room 303, representing the University of Colorado, spoke to the collaboration between the City and the University of Colorado (CU). He voiced his concern that CU may be impacted by the Annexation By Vote initiative and noted that the University is very interested in the Council discussion regarding the initiative.
10. Robert Sharpe, 4995 Marshall Dr., spoke to the importance of preserving Marshall Mesa. He voiced his outrage that the entire side of the Mesa has been slashed to provide a trail for a small amount of people. He suggested that Council rethink its values.
11. Colin Berry, PO Box 1462, a representative of the United States Humane Society, voiced his opposition to the May 23, 2006 Study Session Summary on the prairie dog component of the Urban Wildlife Management Plan.
12. Lynne Segal, 538 Dewey, spoke of the need to provide additional funding for state education and her support of Claire Levy for State Representative. She then asked for candid information regarding the Flatirons Property.
13. Brigitte Mars, 1919 D 19<sup>th</sup> Street, spoke to the need to raise awareness about fluoride. She noted that fluoride was considered a contaminant before it was discovered to prevent cavities. She indicated that the negative impacts of fluoride outweigh the benefits.

### **City Attorney Response:**

City Attorney Calonne clarified that city staff are not partisans on the issue of the Clean Drinking Water initiative, rather, staff provided Council with information relative to the City's ability to implement the initiative.

### **City Manager Response:**

City Manager Bruno spoke to the following:

1. He expressed his appreciation for the level of collaboration between CU and the city, which is largely due to efforts from Vice-Chancellor Tabolt.
2. He clarified that Item 3D was a factual summary of the May 23, 2006 study session, not an action item relating to prairie dogs.

An update on plague control and on prairie dog management was provided.

## **ORDER OF BUSINESS**

- 5. **PUBLIC HEARINGS:**

- A. SECOND READING AND CONSIDERATION OF ORDINANCE No. 7475 AMENDING TITLE 10, CHAPTER 13 HISTORIC PRESERVATION, AND SECTION 4-20-37, “HISTORIC PRESERVATION FEES” B.R.C. 1981, BY AMENDING THE DEFINITIONS, THE PUBLIC PROCESS FOR DESIGNATION OF HISTORIC DISTRICTS, REVIEW OF DEMOLITION PERMITS FOR NON-LANDMARKED BUILDINGS OVER 50 YEARS OLD, FEES AND OTHER REQUIREMENTS. – 7:27 p.m..**

The public hearing was opened:

{3}------------------------------------------------

1. Betty Chronic, 4705 Shawnee Pl., a member of the city task force, noted that the balance between owner and community can be found in Option B, and a 50 year benchmark is a standard to maintain.
2. Susan Balint (no address given) commented that the Historic Preservation revision was “mucked up.” She found Sections 10-13- 4, 5, and 6 difficult to follow and was not in favor of the ordinance.

City Attorney Calonne encouraged Council to deal with policy issues and noted that once those issues are addressed, a rewrite for readability can be completed.

3. Steve Clason, 3305 Eastman Ave., asked the question, “When is it right to make a historic designation over the rights of the property owner?” He voiced his support for the staff recommendation.
4. Kathryn Barth, 2940 20<sup>th</sup> St., a preservation architect, spoke in support of the staff recommendation of Option B and the demolition review recommendations.
5. Robert Sharpe, 5995 Marshall, indicated that citizens gave the property rights to the City when the City was incorporated. He expressed many concerns about demolition and indicated that the ordinance was nowhere near ready.
6. Barbara Anuta, 2605 Regis, Spoke to the importance of Historic Preservation with balance.
7. Leonard Segel, a historic district property owner, noted that he went through the design review process and had a positive experience. He voiced his support of the ordinance as revised and urged Council support.
8. Rodney Mitchell, P.O. Box 3361, voiced his opposition to the ordinance as currently written. He noted that the ordinance is vague and lacks clarity about owner notice and approval. He voiced his support for Option D and his desire for owner consent.
9. John Walker, 1303 Baseline Rd., voiced his support of owner consent and Option D.
10. Estella Cole, P.O. Box 19737, a former Landmarks Preservation Authority Board (LPAB) member, voiced her support of staff’s recommendation of Option B and the demolition review recommendation.
11. Jerry Van Sickle, 617 College Ave., spoke of the importance of preserving property rights.
12. Don Wilson, P.O. Box 1046, spoke to the impacts on property owners when their property is encumbered by specific restrictions. He noted that it is only fair that property owners be allowed to vote on the restrictions and voiced his support for Option D.

{4}------------------------------------------------

13. Ronald Grey, 717 10<sup>th</sup> St., reminded Council that the proposed historic district on University Hill, a couple of years ago, would have encompassed 900 homes. This district was proposed by a handful of people who all resided on one block. Thankfully the community was outraged and the proposal failed. He voiced his support of owner consent.
14. Kathleen Calongne, (no address given) expressed her confusion about how Council could vote on an ordinance and then have the format completely changed later. She suggested that Council should trust the people who elected them. She voiced her support of owner consent.
15. Joyce A. Davies, 4475 Laguna Pl #207, a Boulder resident since 1958, spoke to the importance of historic preservation. As co-president of Historic Boulder, she urged Council to be strong and hold firm. She voiced her support of the staff recommendation.
16. John Sand, 620 Main Street, Gold Hill, spoke of the social contract when one enters an urban community. He voiced his support of the intent of the staff recommended ordinance.
17. Lara Ramsey, 7713 N. 41<sup>st</sup>, Niwot, voiced her support of Option B, staff's recommendation.
18. Sheila Horton, 2940 Island Dr., noted that 2050 properties are included in the map that was distributed. She expressed her concern that the outreach relative to the process was very poor, and that Council is being asked to take action on the ordinance with knowledge that the entire format and process would be changed after the fact. There is no protection of property rights evident in this ordinance. She voiced her support of owner consent through a vote.
19. Margaret Hansen, an architect responsible for numerous restorations and remodels of landmarked properties, noted that elected officials are responsible for establishing zoning, which is the same as landmarking. She voiced her support of the staff recommendation.
20. John Pugh, noted that he supports historic preservation but, as a property owner, wants to preserve the ability to change. He supported Option D.
21. Fred Rubin, spoke of the fundamental provisions of the United States, which protect the individual rights of its citizens. He noted that Council took an oath to support the Constitution of the United States including Amendment 5, which guarantees individual property rights.

{5}------------------------------------------------

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Schultheiss moved, seconded by Gray to adopt Ordinance No. 7475 amending Title 10, Chapter 13 Historic Preservation, and Section 4-20-37, "Historic Preservation Fees" B.R.C. 1981, by amending the definitions, the public process for designation of historic districts, review of demolition permits for non-landmarked buildings over 50 years old, fees and other requirements as outlined in Option B and including the demolition review recommendation of the memo.

Councilmember Ageton offered a substitute motion, seconded by Polk, to accept the ordinance with Option E minus the super majority for the Landmarks Preservation Advisory Board (LPAB) and six votes required from Council.

Mayor Ruzzin offered a friendly amendment to bring additional clarity regarding the role of property owners in developing those guidelines. The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment to the substitute motion.

Mayor Ruzzin offered a friendly amendment to bring back the ordinance with more specificity regarding the criteria for the process around the questionnaire and how it applies to the application. The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment to the substitute motion.

Vote was taken on the substitute motion to accept the ordinance with Option E minus the super majority for the LPAB and six votes required from Council as amended. The motion failed, 2:5, Ageton and Polk in favor, Eldridge and Stoakes absent.

Mayor Ruzzin then offered a friendly amendment to the main motion to bring additional clarity regarding the role of property owners in developing those guidelines. The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment to the main motion.

Mayor Ruzzin offered a friendly amendment to the main motion to bring back the ordinance with more specificity regarding the criteria for the process around the questionnaire and how it applies to the application. The motion was accepted as a friendly amendment to the main motion.

Vote was taken on the main motion to adopt Ordinance No. 7475 amending Title 10, Chapter 13 Historic Preservation, and Section 4-20-37, "Historic Preservation Fees" B.R.C. 1981, by amending the definitions, the public process for designation of historic districts, review of demolition permits for non-landmarked buildings over 50 years old, fees and other requirements as outlined in Option B, including the demolition review recommendation of the memo and including both friendly amendments. The motion carried, 6:1, Polk opposed, Eldridge and Stoakes absent.

Councilmember McGrath moved, seconded by Gray, to direct the City Attorney to redraft the ordinance with a simplified format as used in the Landuse Code Simplification Ordinance. The motion carried 6:1, Schultheiss opposed, Eldridge and Stoakes absent.
