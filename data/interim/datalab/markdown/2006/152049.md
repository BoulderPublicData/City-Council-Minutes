# **CITY OF BOULDER COUNCIL PROCEEDINGS May 2, 2006**

## **1. CALL TO ORDER AND ROLL CALL**

Mayor Ruzzin called the regular May 2, 2006 meeting of the Boulder City Council to order at 6:00 p.m. in the Council Chambers.

Those present were: Councilmembers Ageton, Bohannan, Gray, McGrath, Polk, Ruzzin, and Schultheiss. Councilmembers Eldridge and Stoakes were absent.

## **2. PUBLIC PARTICIPATION and COUNCIL/STAFF RESPONSE**

1. Diane Merker, 2727 4<sup>th</sup> Street, representing the Boulder County Troops Home Coalition, presented Council with a draft resolution calling for the withdrawal of troops from Iraq.
2. Carolyn Bninski, 3312 16<sup>th</sup> Street, representing the Boulder County Troops Home Coalition, also spoke to the Resolution for troop withdrawal from Iraq. She noted the importance of having a balanced panel at the special meeting on June 3<sup>rd</sup>, and feels that a broader viewpoint is needed.
3. Matthew McAllister, 1149 Harper Lake Drive, Louisville, spoke regarding global poverty. He urged Council to sign the ONE Declaration calling for the United States to commit to doing everything in its power to promote the welfare of impoverished peoples.
4. Tim Thomas, 2300 Arapahoe Ave #327, spoke regarding housing discrimination in Boulder and other issues, specifically asking when Council would incorporate community sustainability into open space, library hours, and municipalization.
5. Jennifer Abernethy, 1632 High Street, representing Friends of the Pottery Lab, spoke in support of ongoing funding for the Boulder Pottery Lab. She spoke of the efforts to decrease costs and support the Parks and Recreation budget shortfall.
6. Ann Fontenot, 1755 Gillespie, representing the Pottery Lab, spoke to the possible elimination of the Pottery Lab program. She noted several discrepancies with relation to statistics relating to the program.
7. Don Mock, 2334 Bluff Street, president of the Boulder/Dushanbe Sister City organization, spoke of the need for additional fund raising to meet the commitment of a Cyber Café as a reciprocal gift for the Teahouse.
8. Susan Balint, 760 9<sup>th</sup> Street, spoke regarding historic preservation. She encouraged Council to provide a public forum for the community to participate in this complex issue.

{1}------------------------------------------------

9. Mary Ann Mahoney, 240 Pearl Street, provided Council with a letter from Susan Connelly, Executive Director of Chautauqua, voicing support for Item 3F, Ordinance No. 7463 re: banners.
10. Nolan Rosall, 4940 Pearl East Circle, spoke in favor of the staff recommendation with regard to Retail Strategy Item #6A. He also spoke in support of the historic preservation code changes as well.
11. Jan Otto, 960 Lincoln Place, spoke regarding code enforcement resolutions, and asked Council about the progress of the implementation process, June 1<sup>st</sup> was the target date. He also addressed historic preservation, and urged council to bring consensus within the community and allow public input at the study session on May 9<sup>th</sup>.
12. Rob Smoke, 24<sup>th</sup> 19<sup>th</sup> Street, Apt. B, Spoke regarding the April 20<sup>th</sup> Smoke-In, post crack down. Encouraged Council to think carefully before endorsing CU's approach to either alcohol or marijuana use.
13. Michael Smith, 287 Pearl Street, Blg. R, representing the Boulder Arts Commission, spoke in support of the Banner Ordinance.
14. Peter Richards, 470 University Ave, spoke regarding the real estate transfer tax, and asked council to direct the City Attorneys Office's to provide an analysis regarding the potential to de-bruce the real estate transfer tax.
15. Alison Moore, 1645 Gillaspie Drive, representing the Boulder County Arts Alliance, spoke in support of the Banner Ordinance.
16. Pat Shanks, 3345 Broadway, representing PLAN –Boulder County, voiced opposition to development of the planning reserve. He felt that there is no evidence of any need for development and suggested that the door be closed.
17. Lynn Segal, 538 Dewey, expressed her opposition to Big Box retail development of the Palmos site. She suggested low income vouchers for residents who qualify.
18. Lisa Morzel, 2155 Poplar Ave., spoke in support of the zero waste resolution. Raised demolition permits as a concern in meeting a zero waste goal. She suggested relocation as an alternative.
19. Mary Street, 1919 14<sup>th</sup> St. #330, spoke regarding historic preservation. She asked for public input and consideration of property owner positions.

**City Manager Response:** - 6: 57 p.m.

City Manager Bruno noted the following:

1. Regarding the Pottery Lab, he indicated that staff is still in the preliminary stages of developing budget recommendations for City Manager consideration in June.

Mayor Ruzzin clarified that the city had no part in the events of the April 20<sup>th</sup> Smoke In at CU.

### **B. DISCUSSION AND CONSIDERATION OF A RECOMMENDATION ON THE NORTH BROADWAY TRANSPORTATION IMPROVEMENTS PROJECT COMMUNITY ENVIRONMENTAL ASSESSMENT PROCESS CALLED UP BY CITY COUNCIL ON MARCH 7, 2006. – 7:33 p.m.**

Presentation by transportation planner Noreen Walsh.

The public hearing was opened:

1. Rolf Kirby, 3678 Chase Ct., spoke in favor of having bike lanes in both directions.
2. Neal Lurie, 4934 10<sup>th</sup> Street, representing Bicycle Colorado, spoke of the notoriety of Boulder's Transportation Master Plan, however, he pointed out that W. Broadway has a missing link and urged bike lanes in both directions.
3. Sue Prant, 3389 Kickok, spoke in support of bike lanes on North Broadway as recommended by staff.
4. Lisa Morzel, said that it is a long awaited improvement, and a safe alternative is necessary.
5. Krista Nordback, 77 Dellwood Ave., voiced her opinion that an additional cost of \$225,000 for option D was not a viable alternative. She indicated that the staff recommendation was accepted by TAB over all.
6. Kurt Nordback, 777 Dellwood Ave, representing Boulder Bicycle Commuters (BCC), strongly supported on street bike lanes in both directions. Although A is the best option, BCC supports C, the staff recommendation.
7. Dave Newport, 207 UCB, representing CU Environmental Center, spoke in support of staff's recommendation of Alternative C.
8. Jannell Shaw, 3678 Chaie Ct., spoke in favor of the staff recommendation of Alternative C.
9. Peter Roper, expressed his full support for Alternative C.
10. Mary Eberle, 1520 Cress Ct., representing Norwood neighbors, urged Council to keep 2 lanes open for vehicular traffic both ways. She read several statements from neighbors opposed to losing a south bound lane on N. Broadway.

{5}------------------------------------------------

11. Robert Noyes, 2439 10<sup>th</sup> Street, spoke to the merit of Alternative C., urged support of staff recommendation.
12. Gary Spring, 3675 Aspen Ct., Linden area resident who is both a car user and a cyclist, spoke in support of Alternative C.
13. Anthony Hannigan, a cyclist, spoke of the danger of going south on Broadway and urged support of bike lanes on both sides.
14. Premena, PO Box 1035. a 70 year old cyclist, who has biked for 30 years in Boulder, spoke to the historic significance of not planning ahead for cyclists, and also suggested that the city engineers make the traffic lights more pedestrian friendly.
15. Chuck Gray, 620 Logan Mill Rd., a Boulder cyclist commuter of over 2500 miles a year, pointed out his view that a south bound lane is critical because strong cyclists do not belong on multi-use paths.
16. Pat Shanks, 3345 Broadway, representing PLAN – Boulder County, applauded the community for its passion and said that PLAN Boulder County supports the staff recommendation of Alternative C. He also spoke to the importance of respecting the limited transportation budget.
17. Sam Mason, 3535 Cloverleaf Dr., representing Naropa University and Boulder Bicycle Commuters, voiced his support for staff's recommendation, Alternative C.
18. Ray Keener, representing Growth Cycle, one of 350 people in the bicycle industry, and one of 30,000 regular cyclist commuters, spoke to the importance of increasing the transportation goals of multi model transportation, and support of Alternative C.
19. Andy Hampsten, a bicycle racer who has ridden in the Tour de France, spoke to the importance of fostering a Boulder Bicycle friendly community, and voiced his support of Alternative C.
20. Landon Hillierd, 6500 Arapahoe Ave., Boulder Valley School District Student Transportation Coordinator, said that "it's all about safety!" and that Alternative C is the safest option.
21. Rich Points, an eight year, car-free, resident, spoke to the importance of promoting bike safety and providing connections to N. Broadway.
22. Kate Gardner, 1155 Juniper, chair of Walk Boulder, spoke in support of Alternative C, pedestrian safety depends on separate on-street bike lanes.
23. Dottie Kreider, Oak Circle, indicated that several streets are impacted by losing a south bound lane left of Norwood. She suggested eliminating a north-bound lane sooner to allow for maintaining 2 south-bound lanes.
24. Bill Eberle, Indicated that making it safer for bikes but worse for cars is not a fair trade-off. It should be safe for all.

There being no further speakers the public hearing was closed.

{6}------------------------------------------------

Councilmember Schultheiss moved, seconded by McGrath to approve the North Broadway Transportation Improvements Project Community Environmental Assessment Process as proposed by staff with alternative 5C, and directing staff to come back one year after construction, to report on safety and speed. The motion carried 7:0, Eldridge and Stoakes absent.

### **3D. CONSIDERATION OF A MOTION ADOPTING A REVISED ZERO WASTE RESOLUTION.**

There being no speakers, the public hearing was closed.

Councilmember Schultheiss moved, seconded by Gray to approve Resolution No. 944, declaring Boulder a Zero Waste Community, as amended using the Hotline submitted language with the following changes. The last sentence of paragraph 1 of the “Now therefore...” language, “In all cases, the guiding principles of the City’s Master Plan for Waste Reduction will be followed. Mandatory programs will be employed only if the infrastructure exists and voluntary programs prove not to be successful.” The motion carried 7:0, Eldridge and Stoakes absent.
